# Classical Chart Patterns — Code-Ready Detection Catalog

**Scope.** Multi-bar price structures detectable from OHLCV alone (no external levels), any timeframe from 1m to daily, targeting NQ/ES futures. Written for a detection engine that operates on (a) a stream of confirmed swing pivots and (b) raw OHLC bars. Every entry states geometry in pivot/OHLC terms, an *emerging* (forming) state and a *confirmed* state, tradable levels, Bulkowski's stock/daily statistics, MTF notes, and detection pitfalls.

**Sources.** Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns* (2nd ed. 2005, 3rd ed. 2021) and thepatternsite.com (stat pages "updated 8/24/2020" unless noted); Edwards & Magee, *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends*; Schabacker, *Technical Analysis and Stock Market Profits*; Kirkpatrick & Dahlquist, *Technical Analysis* (ch. 15–16); StockCharts ChartSchool; Investopedia; Lo–Mamaysky–Wang (2000); Leigh et al. (2002); Chang & Osler (1999); Osler (1998); Savin–Weller–Zvingelis (2007); Cervelló-Royo et al. (2015); Arévalo et al. (2017); Tsinaslanidis & Kugiumtzis (2014); Zapranis & Tsinaslanidis (2012); Suh–Li–Gao (2008). Where thepatternsite and the Encyclopedia editions disagree, both are given (thepatternsite = latest); numbers marked "≈" are recalled from the 2nd-edition tables and should be treated as ±2pp.

**Statistics caveat (read once, applies everywhere).** ALL Bulkowski numbers are US stocks, daily bars, mostly 1991–2019, hand-identified, "bull market" unless noted. Break-even failure rate (BEFR) = % of patterns whose post-breakout move is < 5% before reversing. "Average rise/decline" is the ultimate move to the trend-change point (a 20% reversal), not a fixed-horizon return — it is NOT a strategy expectancy. "% meeting target" is for the standard measure rule (full height). Throwback (after up-breakout) / pullback (after down-breakout) rates are ~50–70% and, per Bulkowski, throwbacks *hurt* subsequent performance on average. None of this has been validated on intraday index futures; the academic literature (§B, and per-entry) finds pattern *identification* is real (conditional return distributions differ) but *profitability after costs* is weak or absent, especially in liquid index products. Treat every stat below as a prior to be tested by the engine, not as an edge.

**Global conventions used in entries.**
- `P_i` = swing pivot i (high `H` or low `L`) with `(t_i, price_i)`. Pivots come from the substrate in §A and are only *confirmed* `k` bars after they print (right-strength `k`), which the engine MUST account for (§A.1).
- `ATR` = ATR(14) on the detection timeframe unless stated. Price tolerances are expressed as `tol = max(a·ATR, b·price)`; defaults `a≈0.5–1.0`, `b≈0.15–0.5%` for NQ intraday, `b≈1–3%` daily (Bulkowski uses ~3% for peak/valley equality on daily stocks; Lo–Mamaysky–Wang use 1.5%).
- `H_pat` = pattern height = max(high) − min(low) over the pattern span (or the height at breakout point for triangles/wedges — specified per entry).
- Breakout = **close** beyond the boundary (Bulkowski uses close beyond trendline/pivot); intraday, require a close and optionally `close − level ≥ 0.1–0.25·ATR` or 1 tick beyond plus a filter to reject 1-tick pokes. Bulkowski's "measure rule" targets are stated; % met is his stock/daily rate.
- "Prior trend" is quantified in §A.3; a pattern that needs one states `PT_up`/`PT_down` requirement.
- Duration is given in **bars** (timeframe-agnostic) with Bulkowski's daily-day guidance in parentheses; scaling rules in §A.4.

# §A. Common substrate

Every pattern below is a predicate over (1) a pivot sequence and (2) OHLC bars between/around those pivots. Get this layer right first; every pitfall in §C is downstream of it.

## A.1 Swing pivot detection

Three interchangeable pivot generators; the engine should support all three behind one interface `pivots(bars, params) → [{i, t, price, kind: 'H'|'L', confirmedAt: i+k}]`, alternating H/L (enforce alternation by keeping the more extreme of two same-kind neighbours).

1. **Fractal / N-bar strength (Williams fractal generalized).** Bar `i` is a swing high if `high[i] > high[i-j]` for `j=1..kL` and `high[i] ≥ high[i+j]` for `j=1..kR` (use `>` on left, `≥` on right or vice versa to break ties deterministically). Confirmed at bar `i+kR`. Typical `kL=kR=2..5` (1m–15m), `3..7` (1h–daily). Zero price parameter → produces many micro-pivots in chop; combine with a min-move filter (`|price − last_pivot| ≥ m·ATR`, `m≈0.5–1.5`).
2. **ZigZag (percent or ATR reversal).** Track running extreme; a new opposite pivot is confirmed when price retraces `≥ θ` from the running extreme, `θ = max(pct·price, mult·ATR)`. The pivot's *time* is the extreme bar; its *confirmation* is the bar where the retrace threshold was crossed → look-ahead lag of unknown length. Standard for pattern work because it enforces minimum amplitude, so pattern legs have comparable scale. Typical `mult` 1.5–3 ATR (intraday NQ 1m: ~15–40 pts; 5m: 30–80 pts), or `pct` 0.3–1% intraday, 3–8% daily.
3. **Perceptually Important Points (PIP, Chung/Fu et al.).** Recursively add the point with max perpendicular (or vertical) distance from the current piecewise-linear approximation until `n` points or a distance floor. Best for *template matching* over a fixed window (flags, HS templates); worst for streaming (points can be re-ranked when the window slides). Use for the LMW-style "kernel-smoothed extrema" alternative too: smooth with a Nadaraya–Watson kernel (bandwidth `h` from cross-validation ×0.3 as LMW did) and take local extrema of the smooth — but note the smoother is two-sided, so it is look-ahead by construction; only usable in research with proper confirmation delay, never as-is in live.

**Non-negotiable engine rules**
- Every pivot carries `confirmedAt`; a pattern's *emerging* state at bar `t` may only use pivots with `confirmedAt ≤ t`, and unconfirmed "provisional" extremes must be labelled as such. The classic research bug: using ZigZag output computed over the full history (which knows the future). Bulkowski-style stats implicitly do this (patterns are identified after completion) — fine for descriptive stats, not for a live flag.
- Pivot **magnitude** must be timeframe-scaled (ATR-based), not fixed points; §A.4.
- Keep pivots on the *pattern's* timeframe; nested-timeframe pivots (e.g., 1m pivots inside a 15m flag) are a separate stream.
- Store for each pivot the bar's `volume` and cumulative volume since the last pivot (leg volume) — needed for volume-shape checks (flags/HS/cups) without re-scanning.

## A.2 Trendline fitting

Given ≥2 pivots of one kind, a boundary line is needed for triangles/wedges/channels/flags/broadening. Options, in ascending robustness:
- **Two-touch line** through the first and last (or the two most extreme) pivots; count "touches" = other pivots within `tol` of the line. Bulkowski requires ≥2 touches per side for triangles/wedges (≥3 for broadening/rising wedge on his site: "at least 5 touches total"), ChartSchool ≥2.
- **OLS on pivot points**, or **quantile / hull line**: for a resistance line use the upper hull (line such that no highs exceed it by more than `tol`) — resistance lines should not be violated by intra-pattern bars, which OLS ignores. Practical: fit OLS then shift to the max positive residual (for tops) → "envelope line", then re-count touches within `tol`.
- Slope in **ATR per bar** units so it is comparable across timeframes: `slope_n = (Δprice/Δbars)/ATR`. Classification thresholds used in §C: `|slope_n| < 0.02` ≈ flat (rectangle/asc-desc triangle horizontal side); `0.02–0.15` sloped (wedge/flag/channel); `> 0.4` "vertical" (flagpole/spike). Also store the **angle relative to prior trend** (same sign = continuation-shaped drift, opposite = counter-trend drift).
- Convergence/divergence: lines converge if they intersect in the future within `X` bars (`apex_t`); diverge if they intersect in the past. Report `apex_t` and `width(t) = upper(t) − lower(t)`; triangles need `width` shrinking monotonically ~ (each successive same-kind pivot inside the previous), broadening the opposite.
- Never accept a boundary line that is violated by a close between its two defining touches by more than `tol` (bars slicing through the line = not a boundary).

## A.3 Prior-trend quantification

Many patterns are only meaningful with a preceding move (flags need a pole; reversals need something to reverse; Bulkowski's "trend start" = the point where price had a 20% (daily stocks) counter-move before the pattern). For intraday futures use ATR units:

- `trend_move = (price_at_pattern_start − price_at_trend_start)` where trend_start = last pivot of opposite kind before which a `≥ R·ATR` retrace occurred (Bulkowski uses 20% for stocks; use `R≈3–5` ATR intraday or a %-based analog).
- **Strength**: `trend_move / ATR` (magnitude), `trend_move / (bars × ATR)` (speed, i.e., normalized slope), and **efficiency ratio** `|net move| / Σ|bar-to-bar moves|` (Kaufman ER; poles ≈ >0.6, chop <0.3).
- **Requirements used in §C**: `PT_up(K, M)` = prior up-move `≥ K·ATR` completed within `≤ M` bars; e.g., flag: `PT(≥3–4 ATR, ≤ 20 bars)` with ER ≥ 0.6; H&S top: prior up-move ≥ pattern height (Bulkowski notes HS after short rises perform worse). Bulkowski's "trend length before pattern" bins are short (<3 mo), intermediate (3–6 mo), long (>6 mo) on daily; scale to bars via §A.4.
- Prior trend must be *confirmed before* pattern start; the "trend start" search is backward from the first pattern pivot only.

## A.4 Duration scaling across timeframes

Bulkowski durations are in calendar days on daily charts (e.g., flags "<3 weeks", triangles "3 weeks–3 months", HS "months"). Convert to bars: 1 trading day ≈ 1 bar daily, 6.5 bars (1h RTH), 26 bars (15m), 78 (5m), 390 (1m). Empirically the *bar-count* ranges transfer roughly (a flag is ~5–20 bars whatever the timeframe; a triangle 15–60 bars) — patterns are approximately self-similar in bar counts, not in wall-clock time (Lo–Mamaysky–Wang used a fixed 38-day window on daily; Leigh's flag template used 60- and 120-day windows; intraday flag studies (Cervelló-Royo, Arévalo) used 60–120 bar windows on 15m/1h). Recommended engine approach: define every duration in bars with a `[min,max]` and a *bar-multiplier* per timeframe (default 1.0; allow 0.5–2×), and define every price parameter in ATR units. Sanity constraint: pattern height should be within `[0.5, 6]×ATR×sqrt(bars)` (a random-walk scale check) — heights far below → noise; far above → the "pattern" is actually a trend leg.

Session structure matters intraday for NQ/ES: patterns spanning the 09:30 ET open, the 16:00 close, or the 18:00 Globex reopen contain a regime break; treat overnight/RTH boundary as a hard reset for durations < 1 day, or at least flag `spans_session_break=true`.

## A.5 Breakout definition and confirmation

- Boundary = trendline value at bar `t` (sloped patterns) or horizontal pivot level (tops/bottoms). Breakout bar = first bar with `close` beyond boundary (Bulkowski) — optionally require `close` beyond by `≥ ε = max(1 tick, 0.1·ATR)` or two consecutive closes, or (Edwards & Magee) 3% on daily stocks; for NQ intraday a 3% rule is absurd, use ATR/tick.
- Record `breakout_t`, `breakout_price` (boundary value), `breakout_close`, `breakout_volume / avg_volume(20)`, `gap_on_breakout` (open beyond boundary), and direction. Bulkowski: breakout-day volume above average improves performance modestly for most patterns; gaps on breakout help modestly.
- **Premature breakouts** (a close beyond boundary that returns inside within `≤3` bars, i.e., "false breakout"/"shakeout"): must be modelled explicitly since triangles/rectangles produce them often; Bulkowski counts a "busted" pattern only when price moves ≥ 10% (daily stocks) beyond the breakout then reverses through the opposite boundary (§C4).
- **Partial rise / partial decline** (Bulkowski): inside a triangle/rectangle/broadening pattern, price bounces off one boundary, heads toward the other, but reverses *before* touching it and then breaks out on the side it bounced from — a partial decline predicts an upward breakout (~70–75% for symmetrical triangles/rectangles), partial rise a downward one. Codable: after ≥2 touches per side, detect a leg that reverses at `≤ 60–70%` of the current width and then confirm breakout on the origin side.

## A.6 Targets (measure rule) and levels

Default Bulkowski measure rule: `target = breakout_price ± H_pat` (full height); he reports the % of patterns meeting it and often recommends *half height* for downward breakouts (bear moves in stocks are shorter). Alternatives to expose in code: `x·H_pat` for x ∈ {0.5, 0.62, 1.0}, pole-length projection (flags/measured move), and structural targets (prior swing, apex). Always compute the *ratio* `H_pat / ATR` and `target_distance / ATR` so targets are comparable across timeframes. Invalidation for confirmed patterns is almost always "close back through the opposite boundary" (busted) or "close back inside past the mid-point"; for emerging patterns it is violation of the boundary the pattern was drawn against.

## A.7 Throwbacks / pullbacks (Bulkowski meta-stat, applies to all breakouts)

Throwback = after an upward breakout, price returns to (within ~a bar's range of) the breakout price within 30 days, after first moving away; pullback = same after downward breakouts. Bulkowski: rates 50–70% for most patterns, average time to return ~10–12 days on daily stocks (scale ≈ 10–12 bars on any TF as first guess), and completing a throwback/pullback lowers the subsequent average move (e.g., HS top with pullback: 22% decline vs 30% without in 2nd-ed data). Detection: after breakout, track `dist_from_breakout / ATR`; a throwback is a return to `≤ 0.25·ATR` (or a touch) after having reached `≥ 1·ATR` away. This is a *tradable* second entry ("wait for the throwback") and a re-entry level; it also defines the "did the breakout hold" test: throwbacks that *close* back inside the pattern by > `tol` = failed breakout, not a throwback.

## A.8 Busted patterns (Bulkowski meta-stat)

A pattern is *busted* when, after a confirmed breakout, price moves less than 10% (daily stocks; use `≤ 1·H_pat` or `≤ 2–3·ATR` intraday) in the breakout direction and then closes beyond the opposite side of the pattern. Single bust (then a large move the other way) is Bulkowski's best "pattern": e.g., busted ascending/descending/symmetrical triangles, rectangles, and broadening patterns show average moves after the bust that beat the original pattern's average, while double/triple busts (price whipsaws again) are noise. Code as a state-machine transition on every confirmed pattern: `confirmed → (moved ≥ X) ? active : (closed beyond opposite boundary) ? busted_1 → …`.

## A.9 Volume features (cheap, use as secondary evidence only)

Per-pattern volume shape claims are the weakest part of the classical literature; Bulkowski finds volume trend mostly matters little for performance (a few pp), and index futures volume is dominated by session time-of-day. Normalize volume by time-of-day (same-minute-of-session median over 20 sessions) before testing "declining volume in the pattern", "high volume on the left shoulder vs. right", "breakout volume above average". Expose `vol_slope_n` (OLS slope of normalized volume across the pattern, in %/bar), `vol_at_breakout / median`, and leg volumes.

**Synthesis for the engine.** (1) Identification is a solved, reproducible problem (LMW grammar, Chang–Osler zigzag, Zapranis rules, template scores) — the catalog below is written to those standards. (2) Conditional-return *distributions* differ after patterns (mainly variance/vol regime), conditional *means* are ≈ 0 in liquid indices; H&S/flags/triangles are dominated by trend/momentum filters when both are available. (3) The only intraday index-futures profits in the literature came from exit design (asymmetric, vol-scaled SL/TP) plus a trend filter, essentially independent of pattern fidelity. (4) Every reported "hit rate" (53–73%) is stock/daily or in-sample; none is an after-cost intraday futures direction rate. Consequences: build the detector for *labels + levels*, measure NQ/ES base rates for (confirmation, false-break, throwback, bust, target-hit) per pattern per timeframe on the 1s harness, and always report against a same-side placebo with the same exit stack — exactly the discipline the rest of this repo already imposes.

# §C. Pattern catalog

Entry format: **id** · family · bias — then Geometry, States, Levels, Stats, MTF, Pitfalls. "Stats" are Bulkowski bull-market, daily US stocks (thepatternsite 3rd-ed tables unless noted); ranks are "x/39" for up-breakouts and "x/36" for down-breakouts (1 = best). BEFR = break-even failure rate.

## C1. Continuation patterns (require a pole / prior impulse)

### C1.1 bull-flag / bear-flag
- **family** continuation · **bias** with the pole (up for bull flag; Bulkowski: breakout upward 60% of all flags on daily stocks).
- **Geometry.** (i) *Flagpole*: an impulse leg `PT(K, M)` — Bulkowski: "unusually steep, lasting several days" (his median flagpole ~11 days / +19% for pennants); code: net move `≥ K·ATR` (K≈3–5 intraday, larger for daily) within `≤ M` bars (M≈5–20), efficiency ratio ≥ 0.6, ideally containing ≥1 wide-range bar (range ≥ 1.5·ATR) or a gap. (ii) *Flag body*: a short channel bounded by two roughly **parallel** lines (`|slope_upper − slope_lower| ≤ 0.02 ATR/bar` normalized) that **tilts against the pole** (bull flag: both lines drifting down, `slope_n ∈ [−0.15, −0.005]`; a flat "rectangle flag" is allowed; a flag tilting *with* the pole is a weaker/"pennant-like" variant and Bulkowski finds it underperforms), formed by ≥ 2 pivot highs and ≥ 2 pivot lows (fractal `k=1–2` on the detection TF, since flags are short); body height `≤ 0.5 × pole` (typ. 0.25–0.5) and body height `≥ 1·ATR` (else it is just a pause bar sequence); duration `< 3 weeks` on daily (Bulkowski) → `5–20` bars generally, hard max ~25 bars (longer ⇒ rectangle/channel); retrace of the pole `≤ 50%` (deeper ⇒ not a flag; Leigh's template and most intraday studies use ≤ 38–50%). (iii) Volume: declines through the body in 74–77% of cases; breakout volume above average is helpful but not required. (iv) "Tight vs loose": tight = closes hug the channel, no closes outside lines by > tol, no large white space; Bulkowski: tight beats loose.
- **States.** *Emerging*: pole detected (impulse leg completed as measured to its last confirmed extreme) AND ≥ 3 bars of counter-drift with all closes inside `[pole_top − 0.5·pole, pole_top + tol]` → `candidate_bull_flag_forming` (publish the provisional upper channel line from the two most recent pivot highs / bar highs); state upgrades to `flag_formed` when ≥2 highs and ≥2 lows are confirmed and the parallel/tilt/duration/retrace predicates hold. *Confirmed*: close above the upper flag line (bull) / below lower line (bear); Bulkowski also accepts a close above the flag's high. Failure states: retrace > 50–62% of the pole (void → possibly a deeper pullback / measured-move correction), duration > max (relabel rectangle/channel), or a close through the opposite line (bull flag breaking DOWN — a "flag failure", tradable in itself; Bulkowski counts direction-agnostic flags).
- **Levels.** Entry trigger = upper channel line value at bar t (bull) or flag high; invalidation = flag low (bull) — E&M / practitioners put the stop under the flag's lowest low or, tighter, under the last swing low in the flag; busted = close below the flag low after breakout. Target: **half-staff / measured move**: `target = flag_low + pole_height` (Bulkowski uses flag bottom + pole × 46% for flags, ×35% for pennants; classical = flag breakout + full pole; the *conservative* practitioner target is breakout + pole/2 which is close to Bulkowski's %-met rule). Secondary levels: 50% and 62% retrace of the pole (deeper-flag boundaries), pole high (first resistance/"break of the flag high" trigger).
- **Stats.** Bulkowski flags (unranked, swing-move basis): BEFR 44% up / 45% down; avg move +9% / −8%; meets target 46% / 46%; volume down 74–77%; ~60% break upward. Note "avg rise" here is measured to the *end of the swing*, not to an ultimate high, so it is not comparable with ranked patterns; Bulkowski: "flags and pennants underperform" but are frequent. Half-staff observation: post-flag move ≈ pre-flag move on average but exceeds it only ~30% of the time (pennants). Academic: Leigh, Modani, Purvis & Roberts (2002/2004) — bull-flag *template matching* (10×10 grid weight template on a 120-day window fitting a "pole then downward-drifting consolidation" shape; fit ≥ threshold → buy, hold 20/40/80/100 days) on the NYSE Composite 1980–99 gave excess returns vs. buy-and-hold in-sample; later replications (Wang & Chan 2007 tech stocks/TAIEX; Cervelló-Royo, Guijarro & Michniuk 2015 on DJIA *intraday* (15m/1h) 2000–13 with a risk-averse stop/target rule; Arévalo, García, Guijarro & Peris 2017 dynamic flag detection on intraday DJIA/S&P/…) report positive but modest, parameter-sensitive results, generally not robust to transaction costs at the tightest horizons; the template approach is worth porting as a *feature* (fit score) rather than as a rule. Bulkowski intraday: none.
- **MTF.** A 1m/5m flag is very often the *first pullback* inside a 15m/1h impulse leg; the flag's target (pole projection) usually coincides with the higher-TF measured-move or with a prior HTF pivot — record which HTF structure the pole belongs to (leg 1 of a measured move, breakout from a HTF base, etc.). Consecutive flags in one trend ("flag ladder") = HTF channel; the third+ flag in a run has a materially higher failure rate (Bulkowski notes triangles/flags "far up a trend flame out quicker").
- **Pitfalls.** Flag vs pennant (converging lines, §C1.2) vs small rectangle vs falling wedge (falling wedge is longer, ≥ 3 weeks daily, and its lines converge); flag vs. "pullback to breakout" (a throwback, §C4.1 — the discriminator is whether there is a defined base being retested); the pole must be measured from a real trend start (Bulkowski: from the point where the steep rise began, i.e., last pivot low before the impulse), not from the session open; on 1m NQ the pole is frequently a news/open spike — flags after exhaustion spikes fail; parameter sensitivity: retrace ≤ 50% vs ≤ 38%, min body height, and tilt tolerance change counts by 2–3×; and the "tight/loose" quality metric (fraction of closes within lines, mean |residual|/ATR) is what Bulkowski says matters — expose it as a score.

### C1.2 pennant
- **family** continuation · **bias** with the pole (57% up on daily stocks).
- **Geometry.** Same pole requirement as flags. Body = tiny **symmetrical triangle**: upper line slopes down, lower slopes up, lines converge (apex within `≤ 2×` body length ahead); ≥ 2 pivot highs + ≥ 2 pivot lows; duration ≤ 3 weeks daily → 5–20 bars; height ≤ 0.5 × pole; volume falls through the body in 86% of cases (the strongest volume regularity in Bulkowski's flag family). Bulkowski: pennants that slope *with* the trend do worse.
- **States.** As for flags; *confirmed* on a close beyond the upper (bull) / lower (bear) converging line. Emerging = pole + ≥ 3 bars of contracting ranges (each bar's range shrinking / inside-bar sequence is the 1m-scale analog).
- **Levels.** Trigger = upper line; invalidation = pennant low; target = pole × 35% (Bulkowski up) / 32% (down) added to the pennant bottom / subtracted from top; classical = full pole. Apex time = "expect resolution before ~75% of the way to the apex".
- **Stats.** BEFR 54% up / 54% down (worst-in-class); avg +7% / −6% (swing basis); meets target 35% / 32%; ~1,600 samples. Half-staff: pre-move avg 11 days +19%, post-move 10 days +14%; outbound exceeds inbound 30%.
- **Pitfalls.** Pennant vs. flag = convergence; pennant vs. sym-triangle = duration/pole (a sym-triangle after a pole that lasts > 3 weeks daily / > ~25 bars is a triangle); the apex position matters (breakout before 60–75% of the way is typical, later = weaker); with contracting bars the pivots at k≥2 are often too few — use k=1 fractals or bar highs/lows directly for pennants.

### C1.3 high-and-tight-flag (Bulkowski HTF)
- **family** continuation · **bias** up only.
- **Geometry.** Pole: price rises **≥ 90% ("shoot for a double") in ≤ 2 months** (daily stocks; older study: avg 36 calendar days). Intraday analog: an outsized impulse in the top ~1% of `move/ATR` for the timeframe (e.g., ≥ 8–10 ATR in ≤ 60 bars) — the pattern is *defined by* the extremity of the pole. Then a consolidation that "usually doesn't look like a flag or pennant, just a pause": any tight sideways structure, retrace ideally ≤ 25–36% of the pole (Bulkowski's "tight"), volume receding, down-sloping upper trendline is best. Duration of the flag: ~1–4 weeks daily.
- **States.** Emerging = pole extremity gate satisfied and price paused ≥ 3 bars with retrace ≤ 36%. Confirmed = **close above the highest peak in the pattern** (usually the pole top) — Bulkowski insists on this and not on the trendline break; trendline variant = buy the break of the down-sloping flag top.
- **Levels.** Trigger = pattern high; invalidation = flag low (or below the 50% pole retrace); target = flag bottom + **pole × 0.5** (met 82%; the older 2nd-ed rule used full pole/… with 90% met — page mixes editions).
- **Stats.** rank 30/39, BEFR 15%, avg +39% (3rd ed; 2nd ed +69%), throwback 67%, meets half-height 82%; 1,028 samples. Tips: ~45° pole beats near-vertical; tight beats loose; volume should recede.
- **Pitfalls.** The whole edge is in the *pole selection* (a momentum effect); on index futures a ≥90% move does not exist — the intraday analog is a relative-extremity gate, so treat this as "extreme momentum + tight pause + high break", a family that overlaps the repo's earlier first-hour/pre-close continuation work. Don't accept loose (>50% retrace) or long (> pole duration) pauses.

### C1.4 rectangle (trading range) — continuation and reversal (Bulkowski rectangle top / bottom)
- **family** bilateral (breakout direction decides; Bulkowski: rectangle *top* = prior trend UP, *bottom* = prior trend DOWN; 63% / 59% break upward) · **bias** slight up in his data; treat as bilateral.
- **Geometry.** Two near-horizontal parallel lines: ≥ 3 touches on one line and ≥ 2 on the other (5-touch minimum), price crosses side-to-side (fills the box, no white space); `|slope_n| ≤ 0.02` for both lines and slopes within tol of each other; equal-high tolerance `tol_eq` (=max(0.5 ATR, 0.15%) intraday; Bulkowski informally 1–3% daily); duration: min ~15–20 bars daily-equivalent (E&M: at least 3 weeks; else it's a flag/consolidation), max open-ended (months). Height `H = upper − lower` should be ≥ 2·ATR (else it's a "tight" pause bar cluster). Volume trends down in 70% (tops) / ≥ 71% (bottoms); Bulkowski: *rising* volume trend outperforms.
- **States.** *Emerging*: after 2 highs within tol and 2 lows within tol (4 pivots, alternating) → `candidate_rectangle`; publishes upper/lower lines and mid-line; each further touch increases `n_touches`. *Partial rise / partial decline* (§A.5) is the pre-breakout tell (partial decline → up-breakout 77–79%; partial rise → down 75%). *Confirmed*: close outside a line (Bulkowski) — intraday, add `≥ ε` beyond and/or a second close. Premature (false) breakouts are frequent: label a close outside that returns inside within ≤ 3 bars as `false_break_up/down` (these are themselves informative — a false break on one side often precedes a real break on the other).
- **Levels.** Trigger = upper (long) / lower (short) line; invalidation = opposite line (busted rectangle if closed through after breakout — Bulkowski's busted rectangles are strong reversal signals); intra-pattern trades: buy lower line / sell upper line when `H/ATR` is large (Bulkowski "if tall"). Target: `H × 78–79%` (up) / `× 54–55%` (down) beyond the broken line; classical = full H; multiples of H (2H) are commonly used as extended targets on ranges; the mid-line is a common throwback level.
- **Stats.** rectangle-top: rank 4/39 up (BEFR 15%, +51%, throwback 66%, meets 78%) vs 32/36 down (34%, −13%, pullback 64%, meets 54%); rectangle-bottom: rank 8/39 up (15%, +48%, 64%, 79%) vs 14/36 down (24%, −16%, 66%, 55%). Tall beats short. Academic: LMW rectangle top/bottom = 5 extrema with tops within 0.75% of their mean, bottoms within 0.75% of their mean, lowest top > highest bottom — RTOP/RBOT show some of the strongest conditional-distribution differences in LMW's NASDAQ sample. Intraday ranges are the natural home of the "opening range breakout" literature (Crabel etc.), which is a rectangle with a session-defined duration.
- **MTF.** Rectangles nest: a 5m rectangle is a "base" inside a 1h flag/pole; the HTF direction is the best directional prior for the breakout side (Bulkowski's own numbers: rectangle *top* after an uptrend breaks up 63%; but the *bottom* variant also breaks up 59% — the up-bias is a stock-market drift artifact, not a rule).
- **Pitfalls.** Rectangle vs. triple top/bottom (fewer touches, only one boundary tested), vs. flat flag (short, after a pole), vs. channel (sloped), vs. broadening/triangle (non-parallel); ATR-scaled tol matters enormously on 1m data; enforce "fills the box" via coverage (fraction of bars whose range overlaps the middle 50% of the box ≥ some threshold); the 5-touch rule is what Bulkowski uses to reject noise — count touches with the same tol used to define the lines; session boundaries (overnight range vs RTH) create rectangles that are really regime differences — flag `spans_session_break`.

### C1.5 channel — up / down / horizontal (trend channel)
- **family** continuation (while inside) / reversal at breaks · **bias** with the channel slope while inside; break against the slope = reversal signal (E&M), break with the slope = acceleration/"blow-off" (E&M: often terminal).
- **Geometry.** Two parallel lines (`|Δslope_n| ≤ 0.02`) with `slope_n` beyond the flat threshold (up: > +0.02, down: < −0.02; horizontal channel = rectangle §C1.4); ≥ 2 touches per line (≥ 3 on the primary trendline — the one on the trend's "support" side — is E&M's requirement for a valid trendline; the return line is drawn parallel through the first intervening extreme); duration ≥ ~20 bars (shorter and steeper ⇒ flag); channel width `W ≥ 2·ATR`. Bulkowski treats channels mostly as *trading* structures (his "trend channels" page) rather than ranked patterns.
- **States.** *Emerging*: primary trendline with 2 touches + one opposite pivot → provisional channel; upgrade at 3rd touch of primary line or 2nd of return line. Sub-signals: **failure to reach the return line** (a swing that stalls at < 60–70% of width) precedes a break of the primary trendline (E&M; same logic as Bulkowski's partial rise/decline); **overthrow** (a close beyond the return line that returns inside) precedes trend exhaustion. *Confirmed break*: close through the primary trendline (reversal) or through the return line (acceleration).
- **Levels.** Inside: buy primary line / sell return line (mean reversion) with `W/ATR` sizing; break: entry at trendline value, invalidation = last swing beyond the line, target = `W` projected from the break (E&M: channel width) or the prior swing; for a break of a rising channel down, the measured-move-down target = channel width, then the channel's origin.
- **Stats.** No ranked Bulkowski stats. E&M/Schabacker rules only. Academic: none pattern-specific; trend-following literature applies.
- **Pitfalls.** Channels degrade gracefully into wedges (lines converge) or broadening (diverge) — compute `Δslope` and hand off; ATR-normalized slope thresholds are TF-dependent; the return line is the *weaker* line and touching it is not required (E&M) — do not reject channels because the return line has 1 touch; a channel-break in the trend direction ("upside break of an up-channel") is a *warning* per E&M, not a continuation — code it as `channel_overthrow`.

### C1.6 measured-move-up / measured-move-down (ABCD, "swing measured move")
- **family** continuation (a two-leg trend with a corrective phase) · **bias** with leg 1.
- **Geometry.** Pivots `A (start), B (end of leg 1), C (end of correction), D (target/end of leg 2)`. Leg 1 = `A→B` impulse (`|B−A| ≥ 3–4·ATR`, ER ≥ 0.5, Bulkowski: minor low → minor high; MMU is "best after a downtrend", i.e., A is a reversal low; MMD after an uptrend); correction `B→C` retraces `35–70%` of leg 1 in classical usage (Bulkowski's site: "the computer algorithm looks for retraces of at least 70% — those lead to the best measure-rule performance", i.e., his best-performing MMs have *deep* corrections; his sample averages: retrace 48% over 27 days (MMU) / 48% over 21 days (MMD); expose the band as a parameter and record the realized retrace); correction is *slower/shallower-sloped* than leg 1 (`|slope_n(BC)| < |slope_n(AB)|`) and often takes the form of a flag/channel/wedge; leg 2 begins when price closes beyond the correction's counter-trendline or above the last minor high inside the correction (MMU) — Bulkowski: "buy at the start of the 2nd leg (C)". Durations: Bulkowski averages: leg 1 +36% in 38 days, correction 27 d, leg 2 +31% in 33 d (MMU); MMD: −20% / 26 d, correction 21 d, leg 2 −21% / 27 d — i.e., leg 2 ≈ leg 1 in both length and time (the "measured" part).
- **States.** *Emerging*: after C is a confirmed pivot with retrace in band and price has turned in the leg-1 direction (close above the BC counter-trendline / above the last lower high of the correction) → `mm_leg2_started`; the earlier state "correction in progress" is just a flag/pullback candidate. *Confirmed*: close above B (MMU) — many practitioners require this; Bulkowski enters at C. Void: close below C (MMU) after leg 2 started; retrace > 100% (C < A) voids the pattern (that is a reversal).
- **Levels.** Entry = C-turn / BC-trendline break / B breakout (choose by aggressiveness); invalidation = C (Bulkowski: exit below the corrective low); target `D = C + (B − A)` (full measure) — Bulkowski's %-met: 60% (MMU) / 43% (MMD) → conservative `C + 0.6·(B−A)` / `C − 0.43·(B−A)`; secondary: `C + 0.618·(B−A)`, `C + 1.272·(B−A)` (harmonic AB=CD family, no Bulkowski stats); a *larger* correction retrace → higher chance of meeting the target (Bulkowski).
- **Stats.** No BEFR/rank/throwback (not a breakout pattern); MMU meets 60%, MMD 43% (>1,000 / >950 samples).
- **MTF / pitfalls.** The measured move is the *macro* form of the flag (a flag is an MM with a short, tight correction) and the skeleton of Elliott 1-2-3 / ABCD; leg-2 detection overlaps with "trend continuation after first pullback" — the value is the *target* rule (D) rather than the entry; retrace band and the leg-1 minimum drive counts; on 1m NQ nearly every trend day is a chain of MMs — the engine should emit them at two pivot scales and record `leg2/leg1` ratio outcome to calibrate the target on futures. Overshoots of D by ≥ 0.5×leg1 are Bulkowski's "measured move ... often the second leg is where the pattern fails" caveat; the D level is also where flag-ladders end.

### C1.7 scallops (ascending, descending, and the inverted variants — Bulkowski)
Four J-shaped patterns; the "J" is a rounded turn with one straight side. All are essentially *rounded pullback / rounded bump* structures within a trend.
- **ascending-scallop** (bullish, in an uptrend): "J": left peak → rounded valley → higher right peak (`H_right > H_left`), rounded (not V) bottom (require ≥ 3 sub-pivots or a low-curvature fit of the valley, i.e., the valley bars' lows fit a parabola/circle with small residual; the descent into the valley is the "straight" side); duration ~ 20–60 bars daily-scale; scallops *narrow toward trend end* (each successive scallop shorter/narrower = exhaustion tell). Confirmation: close above the highest peak (up) or below the pattern low (down). Levels: stop below the lowest valley; target `(highest peak − lowest valley) × 62%` above the highest peak (up) / `× 29%` below the lowest valley (down). Stats: up rank 20/39, BEFR 11%, +42%, throwback 68%, meets 62%; down 22/36, 23%, −15%, pullback 64%, 29%; >1,600 samples. A "handle" forming at the right lip = extra entry (cup-and-handle overlap).
- **descending-scallop** (bearish, in a downtrend; also appears in uptrend retraces): backward-J: left peak higher, rounded valley, right peak lower; breaks down 78% of the time. Confirmation: close below the lowest valley (down) / above the right lip (up). Levels: target `(A − B) × 34%` below B (down) / `× 52%` above C (up); stops above the right peak (down) / below the valley (up). Stats: down rank 12/36, BEFR 18%, −16%, pullback 65%, meets 34%; up 29/39, 14%, +39%, 67%, 52%; >2,000. Tall patterns better for down breaks, short for up.
- **ascending-and-inverted-scallop** (bullish; "right half of an umbrella"): a straight rise → rounded top → small decline whose right end retraces ~54% of the prior advance on average (avoid 100% retraces); volume trends down 70%; confirmation close above the highest peak. Levels: stop a tick below the right edge (the scallop's end low); abandon if price drops below the pattern start A; target `(B − A) × 64%` above B. Stats: rank 14/39, BEFR 9%, +45%, throwback 66%, meets 64%; 1,776 samples; one of Bulkowski's best 1–3-month post-breakout gainers (11/12/13%). Geometrically this is a *rounded pullback after an impulse* — i.e., a rounded flag; the ~54% retrace distinguishes it from a tight flag.
- **descending-and-inverted-scallop** (bearish; inverted J with rounded top): rise A→B averages 56% of the subsequent B→C decline; confirmation = close below the lowest valley C without first closing above the peak; cover if 67% of B→C is retraced. Levels: target `(B − C) × 29%` below C (down) / `× 62%` above the top (up). Stats: down rank 10/36, BEFR 17%, −16%, pullback 66%, meets 29%; up 9/39, 16%, +47%, 58%, 62%; >1,500.
- **Detection / pitfalls.** Scallops are curvature patterns: implement a "roundness" score (fit a quadratic to the pivot-to-pivot segment; R² high and vertex inside the segment = rounded; a V has a sharp vertex with 1–2 bars) and reuse it for cups/rounding tops (§C3.7). Ascending scallop vs cup-with-handle vs rounding bottom differ mainly in duration, depth, and whether the right lip exceeds the left. Their intraday incidence is high (any rounded pullback), and their stats are undistinguished; low priority for a first engine beyond the roundness feature.

### C1.8 cup-with-handle (O'Neil / Bulkowski) — continuation with a rounded base
- **family** continuation (in an uptrend; Bulkowski: "price rises into the cup start") · **bias** up.
- **Geometry.** Rounded U-shaped cup (not V; roundness score as C1.7), left rim `A_L`, bottom `B`, right rim `A_R` with `|A_L − A_R| ≤ tol_rim` (Bulkowski "near the same price, flexible"; O'Neil allows the right side to be lower/higher by a few %); cup depth 12–33% (O'Neil) — intraday: `depth ≥ 4·ATR`, ≤ ~60% of the prior advance; cup duration 7–65 weeks daily (O'Neil ≥ 7 wk) → generally 30–300 bars; **handle**: a small drift/pullback on the *right* side, ≥ 1 week daily (≥ 5 bars), forming in the **upper half** of the cup (handle low ≥ midpoint of cup; O'Neil: handle retrace ≤ 12–15%, ideally drifting down on light volume, forming a "shakeout" low), handle length shorter than the cup (Bulkowski: handles shorter than the 22-day median outperform); volume: light in the handle, heavy on breakout (O'Neil rule of ≥ 40–50% above average).
- **States.** *Emerging*: cup formed (rounded valley with the right side back to within tol of the left rim) → `cup_formed`; then a small pullback of ≥ 3 bars staying above the cup midpoint → `handle_forming`. Early entry (Bulkowski): close above the down-sloping trendline along the handle's peaks. *Confirmed*: close above the right rim `A_R` (O'Neil: pivot = handle high + tiny margin; Bulkowski: right cup lip).
- **Levels.** Trigger = right rim (or handle high); invalidation = handle low (Bulkowski: stop at handle low, raise as price rises; O'Neil: 7–8% below the pivot); target `(A_R − B) × 61%` above the breakout (Bulkowski) / classical full cup depth; secondary: "inner cups" (smaller cups inside), the cup rims as throwback support (62%).
- **Stats.** rank 3/39, **BEFR 5%** (one of the lowest), +54%, throwback 62%, meets 61%; 913 samples; lowest up-bust rate of any pattern (10%). But on 1–3-month post-breakout gain it ranks only 11th. Academic: Suh–Li–Gao (2004/08) built rule+NN identifiers with no credible OOS return evidence; Wang & Chan (2009) rounding/saucer templates >20%/yr on 7 tech stocks (in-sample); Zapranis & Tsinaslanidis (2012) rule-based saucers: resistance levels beat saucers, edge only in early sub-periods.
- **inverted-cup-with-handle** (bearish mirror): smooth inverted cup, rims bottom near the same price, handle on the right below the cup top retracing 30–60% of cup height; confirm on close below the right rim low; target = handle height below the right-rim low (Bulkowski) — small; cover on close above the handle-top trendline. Stats: rank 6/36, BEFR 18%, −17%, pullback 67%, meets 62%; 556.
- **Pitfalls.** Cup vs rounding bottom (no handle; longer), vs ascending scallop (right lip higher; no handle), vs double bottom Eve&Eve (two distinct valleys); the handle-in-upper-half rule and the "not V" rule are the discriminators; on intraday NQ, cups longer than a session straddle regime changes; a V-shaped "cup" is a V-bottom (§C3.8).

### C1.9 gaps as continuation: runaway / measuring gap (and gap taxonomy)
Gaps only exist on OHLCV where `open > prev.high` (up) or `open < prev.low` (down) — on continuous futures they occur mainly across the 18:00 ET Globex open, weekend, and the 09:30 cash-open surge (a "gap" in the *RTH-only* series). Bulkowski's taxonomy (>1,100 samples, daily stocks):
- **common/area gap**: inside congestion, no trend; closes within 1 week 85% (up) / 90% (down), median 3–4 days. Detection: gap with no PT and inside a rectangle/triangle bounding box.
- **breakaway gap**: leaves a consolidation/base and starts a trend on high volume that persists for days; closes within 1 week only 1%; median time to close 89 (up) / 84 (down) days. Detection: gap bar's open beyond a completed pattern boundary (rectangle/triangle/HS neckline) = breakout-with-gap; Bulkowski: a gap on breakout day improves pattern performance ~2 times in 3. Best near the yearly high (bull) / low (bear).
- **continuation / runaway / measuring gap**: mid-move in a straight-line trend, price keeps making new highs/lows without closing it, high volume; closes within a week 8% (up) / 15% (down); median 45 / 25 days. **Measure rule: the gap sits at ≈ the midpoint (50–52%) of the move** → target = gap center + (gap center − swing low) (up). Detection: PT present (`≥ 3·ATR` in the trend direction before the gap), gap size ≥ 0.25–0.5·ATR, and the trend continues ≥ 2 bars after; classify retroactively (an exhaustion gap looks identical at first).
- **exhaustion gap**: end of a trend, high volume, often unusually tall, *no* new highs/lows follow; closes within a week 60% (up) / 66% (down); median 6 / 5 days. Detection: gap after an extended run (`PT ≥ 6–8·ATR`), gap size large (≥ 1·ATR), then failure to extend within 1–3 bars and a close back into/through the gap → `exhaustion_gap` (this converts an island reversal, §C3.9, if a second gap the other way follows).
- **Levels.** Gap edges (`prev.high`/`open` for an up gap) are the levels: fill = trade back to the far edge; partial fill; the gap as support/resistance on throwback. Runaway target as above.
- **Pitfalls.** On NQ, ~all meaningful gaps are session gaps (overnight/weekend, RTH open); the "gap-and-go vs gap-fill" question is heavily studied intraday (opening-gap fade is one of the repo's shadow sleeves) — do not re-derive Bulkowski's daily-stock gap-close rates for futures; measure them.
## C2. Bilateral / consolidation patterns (triangles, wedges, broadening, diamond)

Shared machinery: two boundary lines from ≥ 2 pivots each (Bulkowski requires ≥ 3 touches on one and ≥ 2 on the other = 5 total for triangles/wedges/broadening; ChartSchool ≥ 2 each), price crosses side-to-side (coverage), classify by (upper slope, lower slope, convergence): `converging`: (flat, up) = ascending triangle; (down, flat) = descending; (down, up) = symmetrical; (up, up) = rising wedge; (down, down) = falling wedge; `diverging`: (up, down) = broadening top/bottom; (up, flat) = right-angled ascending broadening; (flat, down) = right-angled descending; (up, up, upper steeper) = ascending broadening wedge; (down, down, lower steeper) = descending broadening wedge; converging after diverging = diamond. Compute `apex_bars` (converging) or `origin_bars` (diverging), `breakout_pct_to_apex = (t_break − t_start)/(t_apex − t_start)`; Bulkowski: breakouts occur at ~60–75% of the way to the apex; later than ~85% ⇒ pattern "loses power" (E&M).

### C2.1 symmetrical-triangle
- **family** bilateral · **bias** with prior trend in classical texts (E&M: continuation ~ 3:1) — Bulkowski: 60% break up on daily stocks (drift).
- **Geometry.** Upper line slopes down (`slope_n ≤ −0.01`), lower slopes up (`≥ +0.01`); ≥ 3 touches on one, ≥ 2 on the other; each successive high lower and each low higher (strict contraction of pivots — a *lower low* inside voids it); price crosses side to side; duration ≥ ~3 weeks daily (else pennant) → ≥ 15–20 bars, typically 20–60 bars; ends before the apex; volume declines through the pattern in 84–86% of cases (highest regularity outside pennants); breakout at ~74% of the way to the apex on average.
- **States.** *Emerging*: 4 alternating pivots (H1 > H2, L1 < L2) → `candidate_sym_triangle`, publish both lines, apex; upgrade at 5th touch. Partial rise/decline tell applies. *Confirmed*: close outside a trendline (Bulkowski) — E&M want a decisive close (3% daily stocks) or gap; intraday `ε`. Premature/false breaks are common near the apex.
- **Levels.** Trigger = trendline value at t; invalidation = opposite trendline (busted sym-triangle: Bulkowski's best-performing "pattern" — reversal after a false break); target = **height at the pattern start** (`H = first high − first low`, the widest part) × 58% (up) / 36% (down) from the breakout price (Bulkowski); classical E&M target: parallel to the lower line drawn from the first high (for up-breakouts), or `H` added at breakout; apex price is a magnet/retest level ("expect a turn at the apex" — Bulkowski notes price often reverses around the apex *time*).
- **Stats.** rank 36/39 up (BEFR 25%, +34%, throwback 62%, meets 58%) / 34/36 down (37%, −12%, pullback 65%, meets 36%); > 3,000 samples; heavy breakout volume helps; triangles at the *start* of a trend perform best; symmetrical triangles are among the *worst*-ranked patterns in the 3rd ed. Academic: LMW triangle top/bottom = 5 extrema with monotone highs and lows (`E1 > E3 > E5`, `E2 < E4` for a top); TTOP/TBOT show significant conditional-distribution differences in some subsamples only. Kirkpatrick & Dahlquist summarize triangles as "least reliable of the standard patterns" for prediction of direction.
- **MTF.** Sym-triangles are the classic "wave 4" / "B-wave" structure — usually a *coil* inside a HTF trend leg; direction prior = HTF trend; the apex time is often a HTF turn point.
- **Pitfalls.** Sym-triangle vs pennant (duration/pole), vs wedge (both lines same sign), vs. an H&S in formation (a triangle whose middle high is the head); tolerance on "touches" decides everything — 2+2 with loose tol produces triangles everywhere on 1m; enforce contraction with pivot ordering, not just line fits; the breakout side "should" be established before ≈ 75–80% of the way to the apex — after that Bulkowski/E&M say the pattern is spent (label `apex_expired`).

### C2.2 ascending-triangle
- **family** bilateral (classically bullish continuation) · **bias** up (63% up-breakouts).
- **Geometry.** Flat top (`|slope_n| ≤ 0.02`, ≥ 2–3 highs within `tol_eq`), rising bottom (`slope_n ≥ +0.02`, ≥ 2–3 higher lows, each strictly higher by ≥ tol); 5 touches total; side-to-side coverage; volume down ≥ 78%; breakout ~64% to apex; duration 15–60 bars typical (Bulkowski gives none explicitly; <3 weeks daily would be a pennant).
- **States.** *Emerging*: 2 equal highs + 2 rising lows. Tell: a partial decline (a low that stops well above the rising line, i.e., higher-low *acceleration*) precedes the up-break; a partial rise (fails to reach the flat top) precedes a down-break. *Confirmed*: close above the flat line (up) or below the rising line (down).
- **Levels.** Trigger = flat top (up) / rising line (down); invalidation = last higher low (up trade); busted = close back below the rising line after up-break (or above the flat top after down-break); target `H = flat top − lowest low` × 70% (up) / × 44% (down); classical full H above the flat top. Secondary: the flat top becomes throwback support (64%).
- **Stats.** rank 16/39 up (BEFR 17%, +43%, throwback 64%, meets 70%) / 30/36 down (38%, −13%, pullback 63%, meets 44%); ~1,400. Downward breakouts from ascending triangles fail often (38%) → a **busted ascending triangle** (down break then reversal up) is a Bulkowski favourite. Intermediate-term prior rise → best.
- **Pitfalls.** vs. rectangle (bottom flat too), vs. right-angled ascending broadening (bottom flat, top rising — mirror), vs. "three rising valleys" (no flat top required); equal-high tolerance drives false positives (1m NQ produces flat tops at every round number/session high) — require the flat top to be tested ≥ 3 times with intervening pullbacks ≥ 1·ATR to count as touches; a "descending" version of the same tolerance issue below.

### C2.3 descending-triangle
- **family** bilateral (classically bearish continuation) · **bias** down classically; Bulkowski: 53% up / 47% down on stocks (near coin flip).
- **Geometry.** Flat bottom (≥ 2–3 lows within tol_eq), falling top (≥ 2–3 lower highs); 5 touches; coverage; volume recedes 78%, "quite low just before the breakout"; breakout ~61–65% to apex.
- **States / Levels.** Mirror of C2.2: trigger = flat bottom (down) / falling line (up); target `H = highest high − flat bottom` × 50% (down) / × 64% (up); classical full H below the flat line; invalidation = last lower high (short) / busted on close back above the flat bottom.
- **Stats.** rank 33/39 up (BEFR 22%, +38%, throwback 60%, meets 64%) / 15/36 down (23%, −15%, pullback 58%, meets 50%). Bulkowski: busted descending triangles (down-break then reversal up) give powerful moves; if price rises into the pattern it breaks up 63%; upward-sloping volume improves median performance "dramatically". Kirkpatrick & Dahlquist rate the descending triangle among the better performers for down-breaks in stocks; direction is not reliably predictable ex-ante.
- **Pitfalls.** vs three-falling-peaks (no flat bottom), vs right-angled descending broadening (flat top, falling bottom — mirror), vs. rectangle; the "flat bottom" at a HTF support level is common intraday and the false-break-then-bust is more frequent than the textbook resolution — the engine must keep the busted state.

### C2.4 rising-wedge (reversal AND continuation variants)
- **family** reversal (in an uptrend, E&M) / continuation (bear-market rally rising wedge, E&M) — bilateral in Bulkowski · **bias** down (60% down-breakouts; E&M: rising wedges break down "in most cases").
- **Geometry.** Both lines slope up (`slope_lower > slope_upper > 0`, both `> +0.01 ATR/bar`) and converge (apex ahead); ≥ 5 touches (3/2); duration ≥ 3 weeks daily (Bulkowski; else pennant) → ≥ 15–20 bars, typically 20–60; volume down 79% until breakout; E&M add: the wedge takes ≥ 3 weeks and price "should" break down about 2/3–3/4 of the way to the apex; wedge angle: both lines clearly up (a wedge whose lower line is nearly flat is an ascending triangle).
- **States.** *Emerging*: 4 pivots with higher highs and higher lows where the *lows rise faster than the highs* (momentum shrinking); *tell*: failure to reach the upper line (partial rise) or a bearish divergence in leg lengths (each up-leg shorter). *Confirmed*: close below the lower line (down; classical) or above the upper (up).
- **Levels.** Down: trigger = lower line, invalidation = wedge high (last pivot high), target = **the lowest valley in the pattern** (Bulkowski's rule for wedges — i.e., the wedge start; met ~ mostly) or `H × 32%` below breakout; E&M: retrace the entire wedge. Up: `H × 63%`; Bulkowski warns up-breakouts show weakness ~2 weeks after breakout. Throwback/pullback 72% (highest of the family).
- **Stats.** rank 32/39 up (BEFR 19%, +38%, throwback 72%, meets 63%) / **36/36 down — worst-ranked** (BEFR 51%!, −9%, pullback 72%, meets 32%). Bulkowski: wide/long wedges outperform narrow. So the classical "reliable bearish" reputation is *not* supported for the down-break in stocks — the pattern is real as a structure (momentum decay), but the follow-through is small.
- **MTF / pitfalls.** A rising wedge is what the *end* of a HTF impulse (or a HTF flag drawn at the wrong angle) looks like; distinguish from a bear-flag (which tilts *against* the prior downtrend — a bear flag *is* a small rising wedge/channel in a downtrend, the discriminator is the pole and duration), and from a rising channel (parallel). Because both lines have the same sign, ATR-normalized slope estimation error matters; require the convergence to be significant (`width_end ≤ 0.6 × width_start`).

### C2.5 falling-wedge (reversal AND continuation variants)
- **family** reversal (in a downtrend) / continuation (bull-market correction) · **bias** up (68% up-breakouts).
- **Geometry.** Both lines slope down and converge (`slope_upper < slope_lower < −0.01`); ≥ 5 touches; ≥ 3 weeks daily → 15–60 bars; volume down 72–75%; breakout ~61–62% to apex.
- **States.** *Emerging*: lower highs and lower lows where the highs fall faster than the lows; tell: partial decline (fails to reach lower line). *Confirmed*: close above the upper line (up).
- **Levels.** Up: trigger = upper line, invalidation = wedge low, target = **highest peak in the pattern** (Bulkowski) or `H × 62%`; down: `H × 29%`. Bulkowski's "dip" note: after a *down*-break, price sometimes curls around the wedge front and soars (a bust). Upward gap on breakout and heavy breakout volume help; tall beats short.
- **Stats.** rank 31/39 up (BEFR 26%, +38%, throwback 62%, meets 62%) / 27/36 down (29%, −14%, pullback 74%, meets 29%); > 800.
- **Pitfalls.** vs bull flag (pole + short duration), vs descending triangle (flat bottom), vs falling channel (parallel); the wedge's convergence must be measured on pivots, not on a hull fit that a single spike distorts.

### C2.6 broadening-top / broadening-bottom (megaphone)
- **family** bilateral (classically a *top* reversal in E&M's "broadening top" — five-point reversal) · **bias** 60% up-breakouts in Bulkowski for both (drift); E&M's version is bearish.
- **Geometry.** Higher highs AND lower lows (diverging lines: upper `slope_n > +0.01`, lower `< −0.01`); ≥ 5 touches (3 peaks or 3 valleys on one line, ≥ 2 on the other); side-to-side coverage; duration ~20–80 bars; **top**: prior trend up (trend start below pattern start); **bottom**: prior trend down. Volume: irregular; trends *up* in 65–67% for bottoms (unusual — most patterns show declining volume). E&M's classic "broadening top" = five reversals (three ascending peaks, two descending troughs) with the reversal signalled by the break below the second trough.
- **States.** *Emerging*: 4 pivots with H2 > H1 and L2 < L1 → `candidate_broadening`; publish diverging lines. Tells: **partial rise** (a rally that fails to reach the upper line then breaks down) works 52–53% only; **partial decline** works 72–73% (up-break); Bulkowski's "buy at the 3rd bottom-line touch" intraformation trade. *Confirmed*: close outside a line.
- **Levels.** Trigger = trendline value at t (moving away — the lines diverge, so the trigger drifts); invalidation = opposite line (very far — R:R poor); Bulkowski measure: full height added to the pattern top (66% met, up) / subtracted from the bottom (42%, down), or conservative `H × %`. Intraformation: buy lower-line touch when price turns, short upper-line touch — this is the *practical* use of megaphones intraday (they are the "expanding range" of a volatile session).
- **Stats.** broadening-top: rank 22/39 up (BEFR 18%, +42%, throwback 67%, meets 66%) / 28/36 down (27%, −13%, 67%, 42%); broadening-bottom: rank 15/39 up (16%, +45%, 69%, 65%) / 23/36 down (26%, −15%, 62%, 41%). LMW's BTOP/BBOT (`E1 max, E3 > E1, E5 > E3, E2 > E4`) show significant conditional differences (NASDAQ) — broadening tops are one of the few LMW patterns with a *directional* tilt (negative for BTOP).
- **Pitfalls.** Megaphones on 1m NQ = news volatility; almost every high-vol session shows one; the touch count and coverage requirement matter; distinguish right-angled variants (one flat side) and broadening *wedges* (both lines same sign); with diverging lines the "breakout" is ill-defined — many practitioners instead trade the failure-to-reach (partial rise/decline) or the break of the *last* swing.

### C2.7 right-angled-broadening-ascending (RABFA) / right-angled-broadening-descending (RABFD)
- **RABFA**: horizontal **bottom** (≥ 2–3 lows within tol_eq) + rising top (higher highs); prior trend usually up (74%); ≥ 5 touches; volume up 62–63%; breakout up 55%. Stats: rank 18/39 up (BEFR 15%, +43%, throwback 68%, meets 67%) / 25/36 down (28%, −14%, pullback 63%, meets 40%). Levels: up trigger = *highest peak* (Bulkowski uses a close above the top trendline / highest high; target = H (highest peak − horizontal line) × 67% added to the highest peak); down trigger = the horizontal line (target = H × 40% below it, invalidation = last pivot high). Tells: partial rise works 61%, partial decline 80% (very high). Intraformation: buy the horizontal line, sell the top-line turn.
- **RABFD**: horizontal **top** + falling bottom; prior trend either; ≥ 5 touches; breakout up 64%. Stats: rank 19/39 up (21%, +43%, 64%, 65%) / 18/36 down (23%, −15%, 69%, 51%). Levels: up trigger = horizontal line (target = H × 65% above it), down = close below the falling line / lowest valley (target = H × 51% below the lowest valley); partial rise works 47%, partial decline 75%.
- **Detection.** Same as ascending/descending triangle with the sloped side *diverging*; the flat side is the tradable level; classification error between RABFA and an ascending triangle (which has a *rising* bottom, flat top) is a sign flip on one line — implement the 3×3 slope-sign classifier once and derive all of C2 from it.

### C2.8 ascending-broadening-wedge / descending-broadening-wedge
- **ABW**: both lines slope up, upper steeper (diverging), ≥ 3 peak touches AND ≥ 3 valley touches (Bulkowski is stricter here), volume irregular/up 66–67%, breakout down 52%. Stats: rank 23/39 up (BEFR 15%, +41%, throwback 68%, meets 61%) / 33/36 down (31%, −12%, pullback 62%, meets 71% — of the *lowest valley* target). Levels: down target = lowest valley in the pattern (pattern start); up target = H × 61%; Bulkowski: do NOT short the top (bottom line rises); partial rise 60%, partial decline 65%; up-breakouts are continuations 81% of the time, down-breakouts reversals 81%. Invalidation for down trade = last pivot high; note the down-break trigger is a *rising* line, so it triggers on a mere stall.
- **DBW**: both lines slope down, lower steeper; ≥ 5 touches (3/2); volume up; breakout up 72%. Stats: rank 27/39 up (18%, +39%, 62%, meets 83% — target = highest peak) / 29/36 down (35%, −13%, 64%, 32%). Levels: up target = highest peak (pattern start); down target = H × 32% below the lowest valley; partial rise works only 36%, partial decline 79%; long-term prior rise best for up-breaks; stop a tick below the pattern bottom.
- **Pitfalls.** These are the "expanding channel" shapes; ABW vs rising channel = divergence; DBW vs bull-flag / falling channel = divergence + duration; the up-breakout of a DBW is essentially "return to the pattern start", i.e., a full retrace of the decline — the target = highest peak rule is met 83% (best %-met in Bulkowski's tables) precisely because the target is close.

### C2.9 diamond-top / diamond-bottom
- **family** reversal (top after uptrend, bottom after downtrend) — bilateral in outcome · **bias** diamond-top breaks down 54%, diamond-bottom up 74%.
- **Geometry.** A broadening formation (higher highs, lower lows) followed by a symmetrical triangle (lower highs, higher lows) — four boundary lines forming a rhombus, "usually tilted to one side"; each trendline touched once or twice (Bulkowski is lenient); pivots: ≥ 6 (H1 < H2 > H3, L1 > L2 < L3 with the widest point in the middle); duration ~ 20–60 bars; volume down 55–59% (top) / 67% (bottom); prior trend required (top: up, often "quick, near-vertical"; bottom: down, "vertical drop preceding" best).
- **States.** *Emerging*: broadening phase detected (C2.6 candidate) followed by a contraction (both a lower high and a higher low after the extreme) → `candidate_diamond`; *confirmed*: close outside the converging (right-side) trendlines.
- **Levels.** Trigger = right-side trendline; invalidation = opposite right-side line (busted); target = `H (widest high − low) × 65% (top up) / 63% (top down)`; bottom: `× 73% (up) / 55% (down)`. Bulkowski: after a near-vertical rise into a diamond top and a down-break, expect a full return to where the rise started (launch price); as a *continuation* ("half-staff diamond") the post-move often falls short.
- **Stats.** diamond-top: rank 39/39 up (worst; BEFR 21%, +29%, throwback 57%, meets 65%) / 3/36 down (15%, −17%, pullback 58%, meets 63%); diamond-bottom: rank 27/39 up (13%, +39%, 52%, 73%) / **1/36 down (best down-breakout pattern)** (15%, −19%, 67%, 55%); samples 733 / 477. Diamonds are rare and subjective; Bulkowski's own guidance is that they are hard to find.
- **MTF / pitfalls.** Diamonds are typically the *top of a blow-off*: a spike (news/open) then a widening two-sided auction, then contraction; on 1m NQ they appear after 08:30/10:00 ET data releases; the broadening→contracting sequence is easy to over-detect with lenient touch rules — require the widest H/L to be the pattern's extreme and the post-widening pivots to strictly contract for ≥ 2 pivots per side.

## C3. Reversal patterns

Reversal patterns require a prior trend (§A.3) — without one there is nothing to reverse, and Bulkowski/E&M both note the "reversal" label is only earned by *confirmation* (close through the neckline / confirmation level); before that, twin peaks resolve upward ~60% of the time (Bulkowski: unconfirmed double tops "fail" 60% — price simply continues higher) and unconfirmed double bottoms continue lower ~48%.

### C3.1 Twin-peak / twin-valley family (double tops/bottoms, Adam & Eve variants, Big M/W)

Common geometry (all four DT/DB variants share it; only the *shape* of each extreme differs):

- Pivots: `P1 (extreme), P2 (opposite, the "middle" valley/peak), P3 (extreme)`; for tops `P1,P3 = H`, `P2 = L`; for bottoms mirrored.
- Equality: `|P1 − P3| ≤ tol_eq`, Bulkowski: peaks within ~3% (tops), bottoms 0–6% (avg variation ~1% for A&A DB, 0–4% E&A). Intraday: `tol_eq = max(0.5·ATR, 0.15%)` for NQ; expose as parameter.
- Middle retrace depth: `depth = |P1 − P2| / P1 ≥ 10%` (Bulkowski, "be flexible"); intraday: `depth ≥ 2–3·ATR` and `≥ 30–40%` of the prior-leg length. Too shallow ⇒ it is a horn/pipe/flag-ish consolidation, not a double top.
- Separation: `t3 − t1 ≥ N_sep` bars (Bulkowski "several weeks", medians 16–23 days daily; A&E DB ~2 months). Intraday: `≥ 10–15` bars, `≤ ~150` bars; peaks 1–3 bars apart are pipes (§C3.9), not doubles.
- Prior trend: `PT_up` for tops (Bulkowski: short-term prior rise best; long-term rise ⇒ smallest declines), `PT_down` for bottoms; the pattern must sit at the extreme of that trend (no higher high than P1/P3 within the trend for tops).
- Volume: higher on the left extreme, trending down across the pattern (secondary evidence).
- **Confirmation:** tops — close **below P2 (the lowest valley between the peaks)**; bottoms — close **above P2 (highest peak between the valleys)**. This is the "neckline". Adam/Eve variants do NOT change the confirmation rule.
- **Emerging state:** after P3 is *confirmed* as a pivot (kR bars later) and equality/depth/separation hold → `candidate_double_top` with levels {P1,P3 max = invalidation, P2 = trigger}. Before P3 confirmation, only a "second test of P1 in progress" flag (price within `tol_eq` of P1 after a `≥ depth` retrace) — this is the "swing/aggressive entry" Bulkowski mentions (Big M: short at 2nd peak, stop above highest peak).
- **Levels:** trigger = P2 (close through); invalidation (emerging) = close beyond max(P1,P3) + tol; invalidation (confirmed) = close back above P2 by tol → busted at close above the peaks. Target: `H = max(P1,P3) − P2`; Bulkowski 3rd-ed measure rule multiplies H by the historic %-met (see table); full-H target is the classical rule (E&M). Secondary: mid-point of H (Bulkowski notes 50%-height targets are met far more often); the "launch price" (start of the run into a Big M/W) is a full-retrace target hit ~52% for Big M.

Adam vs Eve shape classification (Bulkowski): **Adam** = narrow, pointed, 1–3-bar spike (inverted V), typically ONE long spike; **Eve** = wide, rounded, composed of several short spikes over ≥ ~5 bars. Codable: measure the width of the extreme at `y = extreme ∓ 0.5·ATR` (number of bars whose high/low is within 0.5·ATR of the extreme, or the width of the pivot at a fixed % of pattern height): Adam if width ≤ 2–3 bars (and the extreme bar's range ≥ 1.5× median range), Eve if width ≥ 5 bars with several sub-pivots. Bulkowski's shape stats show Eve&Eve is best for bottoms and Adam&Eve/Eve&Eve best for tops (see table); the differences are modest and unlikely to be robust intraday — implement the classifier because it's cheap, but don't gate on it.

Academic evidence: Lo–Mamaysky–Wang define double top as `E1 max, E1 and E3 within 1.5%, E3 ≥ 22 days after E1, all extrema between them below E1` (mirror for bottoms) on kernel-smoothed extrema and find the conditional return distribution differs from unconditional for several patterns (Kolmogorov–Smirnov), but not a tradable edge; DTOP/DBOT are among the weaker ones in their tables. Bulkowski's own key finding to import: **the pattern is a bet only after confirmation, and most twin peaks are not double tops** — 60% keep rising. Intraday NQ, "double top" at a session high with a shallow middle is extremely common and mostly noise; the depth and separation gates are what carry information.

Big M / Big W (Bulkowski) = double top/bottom with **tall, near-straight, high-velocity legs** into the first extreme (and out of the second): add `PT(≥ 5–6·ATR run with ER ≥ 0.6)` and require the post-confirmation leg to be checked against the same. Confirmation identical to double top/bottom; measure rule = full height; the run's launch price is a secondary target.

Detection pitfalls: (1) P3 slightly exceeding P1 (a "1-tick sweep") — Bulkowski allows tops within 3%; require `P3 ≤ P1 + tol` and treat a sweep-then-reversal as *stronger* (this is where the "deep-pen wick" observations in this repo's dealer-reaction work sit); (2) don't count consecutive same-kind pivots as P1/P3 without a qualifying P2 in between (depth gate); (3) triple vs double: if a third equal extreme prints before confirmation, re-label as triple (§C3.2); (4) H&S vs double top: a double top whose second peak is lower by more than tol becomes "2B"/lower-high, not DT; (5) confirmation on the middle valley can be far below the peaks (deep P2 ⇒ poor R:R) — record `depth/ATR` and let the strategy layer decide; (6) with retail-scale zigzag on 1m, twin peaks abound — enforce ATR-scaled depth, not fixed pts.

### C3.2 Triple top / triple bottom

- Pivots: tops `H1, L1, H2, L2, H3` (bottoms mirrored); `|Hi − Hj| ≤ tol_eq` for all pairs (Bulkowski: "near the same price", middle peak sometimes marginally lower); valleys need not be equal. Separation between adjacent peaks `≥ N_sep`; total duration typically 20–120 bars (Bulkowski: weeks–months, "several weeks apart"). Prior: `PT_up` (tops); for bottoms the downtrend into the pattern must NOT make a lower low below bottom 1 during the pattern.
- Confirmation: tops — close below the **lowest** valley (`min(L1,L2)`); bottoms — close above the **highest** intermediate peak. Emerging state after `H3` confirmed with equality → `candidate_triple_top`. Invalidation: close above max(H) + tol.
- Levels: trigger = lowest valley; target `H = max(H) − min(L)`, Bulkowski multiplies by %-met (49% TT, 74% TB); classical = full H. Secondary: a "lower final peak" (H3 < H1,H2 by more than tol/2 but within tol) → stronger decline; flat-base triple bottoms preferred.
- Stats: **triple-top** rank 24/36, BEFR 25%, avg −14%, pullback 66%, meets 49% (1,964 samples); **triple-bottom** rank 12/39, BEFR 13%, +46%, throwback 65%, meets 74% (>2,500). Volume trends down in ~61–62% of cases.
- Overlap: triple top ≡ rectangle top with exactly three touches and no lower boundary test symmetry; H&S if the middle peak is clearly higher; a fourth touch ⇒ rectangle. Engine: implement `n_equal_touches` and hand off to rectangle when `n ≥ 4` or when the lows are also equal (rectangle needs ≥2 equal lows).
- Pitfall: after a triple test many intraday traders trade the *breakout through the tops* (a "triple top breakout", i.e., resistance breaks); Bulkowski's triple top is a *reversal* pattern that only exists after a downside confirmation. Keep both outcomes as separate labels: `triple-top-confirmed-down` vs `busted`/`resistance-break-up`.

### C3.3 Head-and-shoulders top / bottom (simple)

- Pivots (top): `LS_H, A1_L, HEAD_H, A2_L, RS_H` (5 pivots; the two armpit lows define the neckline). Constraints: `HEAD_H > LS_H + tol` and `HEAD_H > RS_H + tol` (head is the highest point of the prior uptrend); shoulders "near the same price": `|LS_H − RS_H| ≤ tol_sh` (Bulkowski does not give a %; use `≤ 1·ATR` or ≤ 25–35% of head-height); shoulder symmetry in time: `|(t_HEAD − t_LS) − (t_RS − t_HEAD)| ≤ 0.5·max(...)`; head height above shoulders ≥ some fraction: `(HEAD − max(LS,RS)) ≥ 0.15–0.25 × (HEAD − neckline)`. Neckline = line through `A1_L, A2_L`; slope arbitrary but Bulkowski notes down-sloping necklines are marginally better for bottoms and up-sloping/near-horizontal for tops; steep necklines (|slope_n| > 0.15) usually mean it's not an H&S. Duration: Bulkowski "months" on daily (typ. 30–120 bars); intraday 25–100 bars typical.
- Prior trend `PT_up` (top). Volume: highest on left shoulder or head, lower on the right shoulder (Bulkowski: volume trends down 61% tops / 65% bottoms; right-shoulder volume < left in most). Secondary evidence only.
- **Confirmation:** top — close below the neckline (Bulkowski: if the neckline slopes *down* steeply, use a close below the right armpit `A2_L` instead — avoids waiting for a moving line that may never be reached); bottom — close above the neckline (or above the right armpit if the neckline slopes up). Emerging: `RS_H` confirmed as a pivot and constraints hold → `candidate_hs_top` (levels: neckline, RS high = invalidation). Earlier "forming" flag: after `A2_L` starts and price rises but stays below HEAD (a candidate right shoulder in progress) — useful for pre-positioning but ambiguous.
- Levels: trigger = neckline value at breakout bar (or A2); invalidation (emerging) = close above RS_H + tol (a higher right shoulder ⇒ pattern void; a close above HEAD is a definite void); invalidation (confirmed) = close back above neckline / right shoulder ⇒ busted. Target: `H = HEAD − neckline_value_at_t_HEAD` (measured vertically under the head, not the height at breakout); Bulkowski %-met 51% (top) / 71% (bottom) ⇒ his 3rd-ed measure = H × 0.51 / 0.71; classical E&M = full H (met ~half the time). Secondary: the neckline itself becomes resistance/support for a pullback (68% top / 65% bottom).
- Stats: **hs-top** rank 9/36, BEFR 19%, avg −16%, pullback 68%, meets 51% (>2,800); **hs-bottom** rank 13/39, BEFR 11%, +45%, throwback 65%, meets 71% (3,197). Bulkowski: short prior decline (0–3 mo) best for bottoms; higher-left-shoulder bottoms worse.
- Academic: this is the most-studied pattern. Osler (1998) / Chang & Osler (1999) on daily FX (1973–94): HS identified by a mechanical percent-zigzag algorithm with rules for shoulder/head ordering, ≥ 25% (of head-height?) proportions, and a within-N-days entry after neck-break; profitable in DEM and JPY but not the other four currencies, and dominated by simple momentum/filter rules → "methodical madness". Savin–Weller–Zvingelis (2007) use the LMW kernel definition on US stocks 1990–99: HS has predictive power for risk-adjusted returns of 5–7%/yr over the following 1–3 months, but this is on individual stocks and mostly small caps; Lo–Mamaysky–Wang: HS/IHS conditional distributions differ significantly (esp. NASDAQ), no profitability claim. Zapranis & Tsinaslanidis (2012) formalize HS via a rolling-window rule algorithm (5 extrema, shoulders within 15% of each other relative to head, symmetry ≥ 40%, neckline slope bounds, breakout within some window) and find near-random post-break returns on US stocks once the identification is done in-sample-honestly. Takeaway: identification is repeatable, edge is not — treat as a *structure label* for context.
- Pitfalls: (1) LMW's definition (`E1 max, E3 > E1, E3 > E5, E1 and E5 within 1.5%, E2 and E4 within 1.5%`) requires *armpits equal* — that's a horizontal-neckline special case; Bulkowski allows sloped necklines. Implement general with `neckline_slope` recorded; (2) the neckline value at breakout may be far from the value under the head on sloped lines — target uses the head-vertical height; (3) a right shoulder that overshoots the head void the pattern — but a *marginal* higher high (≤ tol) with immediate rejection is Bulkowski's "head-and-shoulders with a higher right shoulder"—rare; treat as void; (4) confusion with triple top (equal-height middle peak) — require the head to exceed shoulders by tol; (5) confusion with a rounding top (many small pivots) — HS needs exactly 3 dominant peaks at the pattern's pivot scale; run at one scale at a time; (6) intraday, the H&S is frequently a *complex* one (§C3.4) at a finer pivot scale — the multi-scale check helps rather than hurts.

### C3.4 Complex head-and-shoulders (top / bottom)

- Definition: an H&S with **two (or more) left and right shoulders**, or **two heads** (rarely both). Pivots: `LS2, LS1, HEAD(s), RS1, RS2` etc.; shoulders should be symmetric (mirror in count, price, and time — Bulkowski: "look symmetric... a mirror image"). Neckline joins the *lowest* armpits (top) / *highest* armpits (bottom); often nearly horizontal — Bulkowski: near-horizontal necklines perform best (top).
- Confirmation/levels: same as simple H&S (close through neckline or the last armpit). Target: `H = highest head − neckline` × %-met (47% top / 71% bottom) or full H.
- Stats: **complex-hs-top** rank 7/36, BEFR 18%, avg −17%, pullback 66%, meets 47% (>650); **complex-hs-bottom** rank 9/39, BEFR 7%, +47%, throwback 66%, meets 71% (933). Volume higher on the left in most.
- Detection: run the simple-HS matcher on the pivot stream and, when an outer pair of shoulders is present around an inner simple HS with the same neckline (within tol), promote to complex; equivalently a *rounding top with 3–5 dominant peaks the middle of which is highest*. Pitfall: many complex tops are indistinguishable from a broadening/rounding top or a rectangle-with-a-spike; require the head to exceed all shoulders by ≥ tol and the shoulder pairs to be within tol of each other pairwise; keep an explicit `n_shoulders_left/right`, `n_heads`.

### C3.5 Three rising valleys / three falling peaks (Bulkowski)

- **three-rising-valleys** (bullish, rank 6/39): three successive swing lows each higher than the last (`L1 < L2 < L3`, each strictly by ≥ tol), valleys *proportional* (all narrow or all wide; don't mix), pattern usually forms in an *uptrend* (it is a "trend continuation reversal": price was falling short-term within a larger uptrend or the pattern marks the end of a decline); volume trends down 64%. Confirmation: close above the **highest peak** in the pattern (the higher of the two intervening highs, or a higher high after L3). Early entry (Bulkowski): trendline through the two highest peaks, buy the close above it. Stop below L3 (final minor low). Target: `(highest peak − L1)` × 57% (Bulkowski) added to the highest peak; classical: full height. Stats: BEFR 10%, avg +48%, throwback 66%, meets 57% (3,061 samples). Detection: this overlaps heavily with an ascending-triangle / rising-channel description; the distinguishing rule is that only the *lows* must be ordered — the highs are unconstrained (they may rise, be flat, or even fall slightly). Reject if `L3 − L1 > 3× the average valley depth` (that's just a trend, not a pattern) — Bulkowski wants the valleys to "look similar".
- **three-falling-peaks** (bearish, rank 21/36): three successive swing highs each lower (`H1 > H2 > H3` by ≥ tol), similar-looking peaks, peaks need NOT lie on a trendline; forms after an *uptrend* (marks the top). Confirmation: close below the **lowest valley** in the pattern; aggressive variant: if valley 1 is below valley 2 use valley 2. Stop above the most recent minor high; cover if price rises above any of the three peaks. Target: `(H1 − lowest valley)` × 23% (!) — Bulkowski's %-met for full height is only 23%, so his 3rd-ed measure uses 23% of height; classical full-height target is met rarely. Stats: BEFR 22%, avg −15%, pullback 66%, meets 23% (2,300). Detection: distinguish from a descending triangle (needs a flat lower boundary; 3FP does not) and from a falling wedge/channel (needs the lows to also fall on a line; 3FP does not constrain the lows other than the confirmation level).

### C3.6 Horn tops / bottoms and pipe tops / bottoms (Bulkowski, weekly-scale spike patterns)

Both are **two-bar spike** patterns defined on *weekly* charts in Bulkowski; on intraday NQ the analog is "two adjacent (pipe) or one-bar-separated (horn) long-range spikes at an extreme". Stats below are weekly stocks; treat as descriptive only.

- **pipe-top / pipe-bottom**: two *adjacent* bars with unusually long spikes (highs for tops / lows for bottoms) that are "longer than most spikes in the past year" (Bulkowski uses "twice the average" informally; codable: both bars' spike lengths (high − max(open,close) for tops) ≥ `k·median_spike_52`, k≈2, and both bars' *ranges* in the top decile of the trailing window), large price overlap between the two bars, extremes near the same price (top variation avg 20¢ on stocks; bottom variation ~1%) — use `|ext1 − ext2| ≤ 0.5·ATR`; the right spike often has lower volume than the left (tops), above-average volume on one/both spikes (bottoms). Prior trend: usually up into a pipe top / down into a pipe bottom; pipe bottoms best at the *end of a downtrend* (short ≤3 mo), pipe tops best in downtrends but not after prolonged declines. Confirmation: close **below the lowest low of the two bars** (top) / **above the highest high** (bottom). Target: `H = pattern high − pattern low`; ×54% (top) / ×77% (bottom). Stats (weekly): pipe-top BEFR 13%, avg −19%, meets 54% (>4,000); pipe-bottom BEFR 8%, avg +54%, meets 77% (>8,800). No throwback rows given.
- **horn-top / horn-bottom**: same but the two spikes are separated by **one bar** (3-bar pattern) and roughly parallel; confirmation = close below the lowest low (top) / above the highest high (bottom) of the 3-bar span. Stats (weekly): horn-top BEFR 9%, avg −19%, meets 54% (843); horn-bottom BEFR 6%, avg +59%, meets 74% (>1,000). Bulkowski: horn bottoms usually do NOT mark the end of a downtrend; horn tops signal a trend change within ~2 months.
- Detection: purely bar-based (no pivots needed): scan for pairs of bars in the top spike-length quantile; the pattern is only meaningful if the spikes stand out relative to the local range (`spike ≥ 1.5·ATR`) and the pair is at a local extreme (highest highs of the last N bars). Intraday: these are the "double wick rejection" candles familiar to futures traders; expect them to be dominated by news/session-open effects. Pitfall: pipe/horn vs Adam&Adam double top: A&A needs a ≥10% valley between the spikes; pipes/horns have essentially no valley (adjacent). Keep the depth gate as the discriminator.

### C3.7 rounding-bottom / rounding-top (saucer / dome)
- **rounding-bottom** (bullish; Bulkowski: acts as a *continuation* 67% of the time — an uptrend precedes; E&M: reversal after a long decline): a gentle bowl over many bars (Bulkowski prefers weekly; daily 30–200 bars), left lip → rounded low → right lip near the left lip; price may "shoot up midway" near the bottom then retrace most (not all) of it (a bump inside the bowl); confirmation = close above the **left peak** (left lip); a handle at the right lip → enter on the handle's down-trendline break; flat base before the bowl ⇒ powerful rallies. Levels: target `(left lip − lowest valley) × 65%` above the right lip; stops below the right lip / bowl low. Stats: rank 7/39, **BEFR 4% (lowest of all)**, +48%, throwback 64%, meets 65%; 990 samples. Bulkowski (2nd ed.) noted the pattern is often too slow to trade — swing sell the mid-bump, rebuy the retrace.
- **rounding-top** (dome): upward prior trend; rims end near the same price (58% end slightly *higher* than they started); gentle half-moon; up breakout = close above the highest high (i.e., the dome is a *continuation* when it resolves up); down breakout = close below the lower of the two rims. Levels: `(peak A − right rim low B) × 58%` above A (up) / `× 14%` below B (down; i.e., down-side targets are rarely met at full height); aggressive buy at a 32% retrace of pattern height; both rims are support. Stats: **up rank 2/39** (BEFR 9%, +55%, throwback 63%, meets 58%) / down 3/36 (20%, −17%, pullback 58%, meets 14%). Underperforms when the right rim > left rim.
- **Detection.** Curvature fit on the pivot sequence or on a smoothed close (LMW kernel or a rolling quadratic): rounding = many small pivots with monotone-decreasing then increasing slopes; require `n_pivots ≥ 5–7` inside the bowl and no single leg > 40% of the bowl height (else it's a V or a double bottom); the two rims within `tol_rim`. Academic: Wang & Chan (2009) rounding-top templates; Zapranis–Tsinaslanidis (2012) rule-based saucers (resistance levels beat them). Pitfalls: rounding vs cup-with-handle (handle), vs complex H&S (three dominant peaks), vs Eve&Eve double top (two valleys) — these are the *same* structure at different pivot scales; emit all with scale tags. On intraday NQ, hour-scale saucers are common in the 11:00–14:00 lull and resolve with the afternoon session, so session timing dominates.

### C3.8 V-top / V-bottom (spike reversal) and extended-V
- **v-top** (bearish): a **straight-line uptrend** with few pauses (often in a channel), 3 weeks–3 months daily (→ ~15–65 bars; intraday: ≥ 15 bars of trend with ER ≥ 0.6), then a sharp reversal — one-day reversal bar, island reversal, or long tail ("tail" = spike bar), on heavy volume; the up-trendline is pierced; the right (down) side retraces `≥ 38.2%` of the left side; **breakout = close below the 38.2% retrace level** of the left side (Bulkowski's confirmation); *no pause* between the V and the breakout (a pause = "extended V"); exclude single-bar spikes (that is a spike/pipe, not a V, in Bulkowski's usage — E&M call the whole family "spike/V"); left and right angles often mirror. Levels: entry = close below the 38.2% retrace, target = the left-side start low `B` (full retrace of the run); tall (height/breakout price > 25%) outperform; wait 3 bars after the trendline break. Stats: rank 20/36, BEFR 29%, −15%, pullback 56%, meets 37%; 2,416.
- **v-bottom** (bullish): mirror — straight-line downtrend, reversal bar/island/tail on heavy volume, maybe a gap up, must pierce the down-trendline, right side retraces ≥ 38.2%, no pause; entry = `low + 0.382·(high − low)` on a close above; target = left-side high A. Stats: rank 24/39, BEFR 19%, +40%, throwback 55%, meets 52%; 1,997. Tall (> 34.7%) better; best in the lower third of the yearly range.
- **extended-v-top** (bearish): V-top followed by a *pause* — a small flag slanting slightly up — between the peak and the 38.2%-retrace breakout; entry on the flag break or below the 38.2% level; target = A (pattern start low). Stats: **rank 2/36**, BEFR 15%, −18%, pullback 63%, meets 49%; 1,217. **extended-v-bottom**: V-bottom then a sideways extension then continuation; breakout = close above the right-side peak; target = pattern-start high. Stats: rank 24/39, BEFR 10%, +40%, throwback 62%, meets 51%; 288 samples only.
- **Detection.** V patterns are *trend-then-reversal-bar* structures: gate (i) prior trend straightness (channel fit R², ER), (ii) reversal bar signature (range ≥ 1.5·ATR, close in the opposite third, or an island), (iii) trendline pierce, (iv) 38.2% retrace close = confirmation; the extended variant adds a flag detector (C1.1) *after* the V and before/around the 38.2% level. Pitfalls: V-tops are indistinguishable from exhaustion spikes at the time of the spike — the 38.2% confirmation is what makes it a pattern; single-bar spikes excluded; on 1m NQ the "V" after news is common and the extended-V (spike, flag, break) is the tradable structure — but this overlaps with the repo's dead dealer-reaction/thin-book work; expect regime dependence.

### C3.9 island reversal (top / bottom) and long island
- **island-top / island-bottom**: an uptrend (top) / downtrend (bottom) into a cluster of bars isolated by **two gaps that overlap in price** (gap up into the island, gap down out of it for a top — the gaps must share some/all of the same price range); island 1 bar to several months; volume high on the second-gap bar. Confirmation = the second gap itself (the bar after the exit gap). Levels: island height `H = highest high − lowest low`; target `H × 82%` above (island bottom, up) / `× 62%` below (island top, down); the exit-gap edge is the invalidation (a close back into the island fills the gap → failed island; Bulkowski: waiting for a throwback/pullback to complete improves results). Stats: **island-bottom rank 38/39** (BEFR 31%, +31%, throwback 54%, meets 82%); island-top 31/36 (34%, −13%, pullback 55%, meets 62%); >2,000 each; busted rates high (island bottoms 21%, tops ~42% indicative). Tall islands better. Bulkowski's verdict: mediocre.
- **long-island**: two *non-aligned* gaps (if aligned it's an island reversal), gaps ≥ some min width, duration < 4 months; islands shorten within trends (reversal warning); breakout = the bar after the 2nd gap. Stats (larger 56/53-pattern universe): up rank 43/56, BEFR 21%, +33%, throwback 53%, meets 71%; down 34/53, 27%, −14%, pullback 52%, 55%; target = island height / 2 (×%) from the close before the 2nd gap.
- **Detection / futures note.** Requires true gaps → on continuous NQ this is essentially a *session* phenomenon (overnight gap up into a one-day island then gap down = a classic daily-chart island; intraday-only islands exist only if RTH-only bars are used and gaps at 09:30 count). Implement on the daily/RTH-session series; on 1m/5m continuous data the pattern degenerates into "spike then reversal" (see V-top). Pitfall: an island requires the two gaps to *overlap* — a common bug is to accept any pair of opposite gaps.

### C3.10 bump-and-run reversal (BARR top / bottom, Bulkowski)
- **barr-top** (bearish, ranked 3/36): a *lead-in* phase — a trendline rising at ~30–45° on the arithmetic chart (Bulkowski; scale-dependent — code as `slope_n` in a moderate band, e.g., 0.05–0.2 ATR/bar) for ≥ 1 month daily (≥ 20–30 bars), lead-in height = max vertical distance from price to the trendline in the lead-in (`≥ 1·ATR·√bars`-ish; Bulkowski "≥ $1, prefer ≥ $2" on stocks); then the *bump*: price accelerates away from the trendline at 45–60° on high volume (often news), reaching a **bump height ≥ 2× the lead-in height** above the trendline; then price rolls over (rounded or spiky top) and declines back toward the trendline (the "run"); extra bumps (dual bumps 18–21%) may form. **Confirmation** = close below the lead-in trendline (extended). Levels: entry = trendline break; **warning line** = a parallel one lead-in height above the trendline (a break of it on the way down is the early sell), further "sell lines" at multiples; invalidation = new high above the bump top; **target = the pattern's start (bottom of the pattern / trendline origin)** — Bulkowski's rule, met 44%; the trendline itself is the pullback level (64%). Stats: BEFR 14%, −17%, pullback 64%, meets 44%; 1,488; lowest downward bust rate of any pattern.
- **barr-bottom** (bullish, **rank 1/39, best up-breakout pattern**): mirror ("frying pan tilted down, handle on the left"): a down-sloping lead-in trendline (0–45°) for ≥ 1 month (avg 35 days), lead-in height measured in the first quarter; then a rapid drop steepening to ≥ 60° with a rounded turn, bump height ≥ 2× lead-in; volume high at pattern start, bump start, and breakout; confirmation = close above the down-sloping trendline (or above a parallel line one lead-in height below the top line — the "warning line" analog); target = **the highest high in the pattern**; dual bumps ~12%. Stats: BEFR 9%, +55%, throwback 61%, meets 76%; 1,099.
- **Detection.** (i) Fit the lead-in trendline through ≥ 2–3 lows (top) with moderate normalized slope; (ii) compute the lead-in height `h_L` (max residual above the line during the lead-in); (iii) detect the bump: residual above the line reaches `≥ 2·h_L` with a steeper local slope; (iv) the bump top prints (pivot confirmed) and price falls back; (v) states: `bump_forming` (residual > 2h_L), `bump_top_in` (pivot confirmed below the bump peak), `warning_line_broken`, `confirmed` (close below the trendline). This is a *parabolic-blowoff-and-reversion* detector — the same object as "extension from a trendline in ATR units"; angle criteria must be replaced by ATR-normalized slopes because degrees on a chart are scale artefacts (Bulkowski himself specifies arithmetic scale). Pitfalls: the trendline must be re-anchored as the lead-in extends; a bump that never reaches 2× is just a channel overthrow; the run may itself contain flags/HS — do not double-count.

### C3.11 dead-cat-bounce (event pattern) and inverted DCB
- **dead-cat-bounce**: an *event* gap down (Bulkowski: close 15–70% below the prior close, avg 31% — earnings/news, stocks); the trend low prints ~7 days after the event (46% make a lower low the next day); a bounce averaging 28% over 23 days; then a decline averaging 30% over 49 days; 67% of the time price eventually falls below the event low (avg 18% lower); the gap is closed within 1 day 22%, within 3 months 38%, within 6 months 58%; a second DCB follows within 3 months 26% / 6 months 38%. Levels: bounce peak = short entry (Bulkowski: short at the bounce peak, target the event low), event low = first target; larger event decline → larger/longer bounce. **inverted-dcb**: event rise 5–20%+, often a higher high next day, then a leisurely decline (sell next day; rebuy after ~day 3 / week 2 depending on size).
- **Futures relevance.** No such single-name event gaps on NQ; the analog is the *post-shock reaction* after a scheduled macro release (CPI/FOMC) or a limit-down/-up session: an outsized move (`≥ 4–6·ATR` in ≤ N bars), a partial bounce (retracing ~30–50%), then continuation. Code as `event_shock → bounce → retest` state machine using ATR-scaled thresholds; the repo's event-reaction work (CPI core) already covers the causal side. Not a priority pattern for the geometric engine.

### C3.12 roof / inverted roof (Bulkowski, rare)
- **roof**: flat/near-flat bottom touched ≥ 3 times, inverted-V top (an up-slope in the first half, a down-slope in the second) — the *upper half of a diamond*; symmetrical halves; usually after an up move; confirm on close outside either boundary. Stats: up rank 35/39 (BEFR 26%, +34%, throwback 60%, meets 62%) / down 16/36 (22%, −15%, pullback 66%, 63%); >300. Target `(peak − flat low) × 62–63%`. Down breakouts better; height/breakout > 8.7–9.2% outperform.
- **inverted-roof**: horizontal top touched often, V-shaped bottom (the *lower half of a diamond*); best after sharp rises; confirm on close outside. Stats: up rank 37/39 (BEFR 23%, +34%, throwback 58%, meets 65%) / down 26/36 (25%, −14%, pullback 64%, 47%); >450; **35% upward bust rate (worst)**. Distinguish from H&S bottom (three valleys vs a single V).
- **Detection.** = a right-angled shape with one flat side and a *two-line* other side (converging then diverging) — the diamond machinery (C2.9) with one side flattened. Low priority.

### C3.13 breakaway / exhaustion gaps as reversal signals; one-day reversal / key reversal bar
- Breakaway gap out of a *base* (rectangle/HS/rounding bottom) = the highest-quality reversal confirmation in E&M/Bulkowski (a gap on breakout day improves the pattern's performance ~2/3 of the time; breakaway gaps close within a week only ~1%). Exhaustion gap (after an extended run, large, not followed by new extremes, closed within 1–3 bars) = reversal warning; two of them (an island) = reversal. Code both as *attributes* of the breakout/gap event rather than separate patterns (see C1.9).
- **one-day-reversal / key reversal / spike** (E&M; the intraday analog is the "reversal bar"): a bar making a new trend extreme with a wide range (≥ 1.5–2·ATR) and closing in the opposite third (key reversal: also opens beyond the prior close and closes beyond the prior bar's opposite extreme), on high volume; E&M treat it as a *component* (the apex of a V, the second gap of an island, the head of an HS on a smaller scale). Emit as `reversal_bar` with the extreme as invalidation and the bar's midpoint / opposite extreme as the trigger; it is not itself a Bulkowski chart pattern (his tails/spikes are covered under V and pipes/horns).

## C4. Post-breakout and meta-patterns

### C4.1 throwback / pullback (after breakout)
- **Definition (Bulkowski).** Throwback: after an *upward* breakout, price moves away, then returns to (or near) the breakout price within 30 days; pullback: same after a downward breakout, returning to the breakout price/trendline. Rates: **58%** of 10,305 up-breakouts (since 2000; ~70% when breakout volume > 30-day average), **58%** of 8,765 down-breakouts (66% on high-volume breakouts). Shape: rise avg 6 days / ~8% (most 4–10%) before turning; round trip ~10 days (throwback) / drop 6 days / 9%, round trip 11 days (pullback). After the return: 65% resume upward (throwback) vs 35% keep falling (= bust); pullback: 53% keep rising (bust!) vs 47% resume down. Effect on performance: patterns *with* a throwback rise to the ultimate high 40% if the breakout price holds vs 29% if price drops below the breakout; pullbacks: decline 25% if price stays ≤ breakout vs 20% if it climbs above. Chief cause: nearby overhead resistance / underlying support (Bulkowski) — a breakout into a HTF level throws back more often; ≥ 3 higher closes before the breakout → fewer throwbacks.
- **Detection (per §A.7).** State machine after `confirmed`: `moved_away` when `max_excursion ≥ 1·ATR` (or ≥ 0.5×H_pat); `throwback_in_progress` when price returns to within `0.25·ATR` (or touches) the breakout level within `≤ 12–15 bars` (Bulkowski 30 days daily → scale); resolve to `throwback_held` (close back in the breakout direction beyond the throwback low/high, or new excursion high) or `failed_breakout` (close inside the pattern by > tol) → feeds C4.2. Record: time-to-return, depth (did it close beyond the level), and whether the pattern's opposite boundary/mid-line was reached.
- **Tradable.** The throwback re-entry (buy the return to the breakout level with a stop inside the pattern) is the "second chance" trade in every classical text; Bulkowski shows it is *lower* expectancy than patterns that never throw back — but as an entry *technique* it has better location than chasing. Pitfall: throwback vs a *new* flag/pullback pattern — the discriminator is whether the reference level is a just-broken boundary.

### C4.2 busted patterns (single / double / triple bust)
- **Definition (Bulkowski).** After a confirmed breakout, price moves **≤ 10%** (daily stocks; intraday use `≤ 1·H_pat` or `≤ 2–3·ATR`) in the breakout direction, then reverses and closes beyond the *opposite* side of the pattern (breaks out the other way). Bust rates: **median 24% of upward breakouts bust, 40% of downward breakouts** bust; extremes: rising-wedge down-breakouts bust 63%, inverted-roof up-breakouts 35%, cup-with-handle up only 10%, BARR top lowest for down. Among busted patterns: single bust (then a sustained move the new way) median 53% (up-busts) / 67% (down-busts); double bust (price whipsaws back again) 31% / 4%; triple+ 16% / 28%. Performance: **single-busted downward breakouts (reversing up) median rise 53% vs 42% for non-busted up-breakouts; single-busted upward breakouts (reversing down) median decline 23% vs 15% for non-busted down-breakouts** — "the move is often stronger from a failed pattern than one that works." Trade the new direction; double/triple busts are noise.
- **Detection.** For every confirmed pattern keep `max_excursion` in the breakout direction (in ATR and in H_pat units) and watch for a close beyond the opposite boundary (for triangles/wedges the boundary line extended; for tops/bottoms the pattern's extreme). Emit `busted_1` at that close with: original pattern id, excursion before bust, bars since breakout; entry = the bust close (or the retest of the opposite boundary), invalidation = the failed-breakout extreme, target = full `H_pat` from the bust point (Bulkowski uses the pattern height for busted-pattern targets), or the original pattern's *far* boundary target mirrored. `busted_2` when price re-crosses back through the original breakout side within `≤ 1·H_pat` excursion — after `busted_2`, stop trading the pattern (rectangle/triangle chop).
- **Why it matters for this repo.** Bulkowski's busted-pattern edge is a *failed-breakout* / trap-reversal edge — the same object as the repo's earlier "sweep-and-fail" and level-reaction findings; the pattern's boundary is the trap level. This is the single most important post-breakout state to get right.

### C4.3 partial rise / partial decline (pre-breakout tell) — summary
Codified in §A.5 and each C2 entry: inside a range-type pattern (rectangle, triangle, broadening) with ≥ 2 touches per side, a swing that turns back *before* reaching the far boundary (Bulkowski: "before coming close to or touching" — code as `< 60–70% of current width` traversed) predicts a breakout on the side it started from: partial decline → up-breakout works 65–80% (rectangles 77–79%, RABFA 80%, DBW 79%, broadening 72–73%); partial rise → down-breakout works 36–75% (rectangles 75%, RABFD 47%, DBW 36%, broadening 52%). Emit `partial_rise/decline` with the width fraction traversed; it is one of the few *directional* pre-breakout tells with decent Bulkowski numbers, and it is essentially a "failure to reach the far side = weakness" rule that transfers conceptually to intraday ranges (opening range failure tests, etc.).

### C4.4 Wolfe wave (brief; harmonic-adjacent)
Five turning points forming a wedge (points 1–2–3–4–5) where lines 1–3 and 2–4 converge (apex = estimated time of arrival, ETA), point 5 overshoots the 1–3 line ("sweet spot" = a line parallel to 2–4 through 3), and the target = line 1–4 extended (EPA). Bulkowski (1990–2019): bull WW 6,269 patterns — 41% reach the EPA, 49% stopped out below point 5, avg rise to EPA 12% / to ultimate high 23%, BEFR 15%, rank 34/39, ~14 days to EPA; bear WW ~6,000 — 35% reach EPA, 57% stopped out, avg decline 12%, BEFR 30%, rank 35/36. Detection = rising/falling-wedge machinery (C2.4/5) + the overshoot at point 5 + the 1–4 projection; low priority (poor stats), but the 1–4 projection is a distinct *target* rule worth exposing.

### C4.5 Pattern-in-pattern nesting (MTF composition rules)
- **Flag inside pole inside measured move**: 1m flag ⊂ 5m impulse leg (leg 1 or leg 2 of a 15m measured move) ⊂ 1h channel. Emit `parent_pattern_id` where a lower-TF pattern's span lies within a higher-TF pattern's leg, and `role` (e.g., `retest_of_parent_boundary`, `leg2_of_parent_mm`, `right_shoulder_of_parent_hs`).
- **Right shoulders are usually a lower-TF pattern**: an HS right shoulder on 15m is often a 1m double top / rising wedge; a triangle apex on 1h is a 5m pennant; the "handle" of a cup is a 5m flag; the "bump" of a BARR is a 1m parabolic channel. Detection at the fine scale gives *earlier confirmation* of the coarse pattern (Bulkowski's "early entry" tips are exactly this: buy the handle trendline break, the down-sloping line over the 3RV peaks, the flag inside the extended-V).
- **Higher-TF pattern boundary = lower-TF level**: a 1h rectangle top is where 1m double tops cluster; false breaks of the 1h boundary are 1m busted patterns. The engine should expose HTF boundaries as levels to the LTF detectors (this is where the repo's LT/GEX-level machinery and chart structure meet — the "level" is now endogenous).
- **Direction prior**: HTF trend/pattern state (inside a rising channel vs. at the top of a broadening top) is the best available prior for the resolution side of a bilateral LTF pattern (Bulkowski's own up-bias numbers are stock-market drift; intraday use the HTF structure instead).
- **Time-of-day**: on NQ/ES, pattern durations interact with the session clock (open-range → rectangle by 10:30; lunch saucers; pre-close continuation) — store `session_phase_at_start/at_breakout` on every instance.

# §E. Detection-engine checklist — what must be right

1. **Pivot confirmation lag is part of the pattern.** Every state transition must be timestamped at the bar where it became knowable (`confirmedAt`), never at the pivot's own bar. Research that labels patterns with full-history zigzag and then measures forward returns from the pivot bar is look-ahead (the same failure class as the repo's LSTB adverse-cancel and FSM resolve-walk bugs). Emit `t_knowable` for every level and only trade at ≥ that bar.
2. **ATR-scale everything, then hold bar-counts roughly constant across timeframes.** Fixed-point tolerances make 1m and 1h detectors incomparable; fixed wall-clock durations do the same. Also add the random-walk sanity band on `H_pat/(ATR·√bars)`.
3. **Confirmation ≠ existence.** Doubles/triples/H&S are not "patterns" until the neckline/valley closes; flags/triangles/wedges are not signals until the boundary closes; and 40–60% of unconfirmed candidates resolve the *other* way (Bulkowski: 60% of twin peaks keep rising). Store both the *emerging* candidate and its resolution so the strategy layer can trade the emergence (mean-revert at the second test) or the confirmation (breakout) explicitly — and so the base rates can be measured on NQ.
4. **Model the failure states as first-class events**: false breakout (close back inside within ≤3 bars), throwback/pullback (return to breakout level after ≥1 ATR excursion), busted (< 3 ATR follow-through then close through the far side), double bust; and partial rise/decline before breakout. In Bulkowski's data the busts (esp. of triangles, rectangles, broadening patterns) and the partial-rise/decline tells beat the textbook breakouts, and in this repo's own history (LT/GEX level work) the "sweep-and-fail" outcome is what carried information. A detector that only emits textbook completions throws away the best half of the signal.
5. **One slope-sign classifier for all C2 shapes, one twin-extreme matcher for all C3.1/C3.2/C3.9 shapes.** Sibling confusion (asc-triangle vs RABFA vs rectangle vs three-rising-valleys; double vs triple vs pipe vs H&S) is a *labelling* choice on the same pivot geometry — compute the geometry once, emit all applicable labels with their discriminating features (`Δslope`, `n_touches`, `depth/ATR`, `sep_bars`, `head_excess`), and let evaluation decide which labels carry information. Do not hard-code exclusivity.
6. **Volume is a weak, time-of-day-confounded feature on index futures.** Normalize by minute-of-session before using any "volume declines through the pattern" rule; never gate on volume in v1.
7. **Session and roll boundaries.** Reset intraday pattern state at 18:00 ET (Globex open) and treat 09:30/16:00 as regime breaks; on raw contracts, force-close pattern state at the primary-contract switch (per CLAUDE.md rollover rules) — a "double top" whose two peaks straddle a roll is an artifact.
8. **Statistics from Bulkowski are priors, not parameters.** They are daily-stock, bull-market, hand-picked, and measure "ultimate move to a 20% reversal", which is not a fixed-horizon return; the academic record (LMW, Chang–Osler, Savin et al., Zapranis–Tsinaslanidis, Cervelló-Royo, Arévalo) says identification is real, unconditional profitability is not, and index products are the hardest venue. The engine's job is to reproduce the *geometry* precisely and then let the repo's 1s-honest harness (CLAUDE.md §"Order Execution and Backtesting") measure NQ/ES base rates for confirmation, throwback, bust, and target-hit per pattern per timeframe.
