## 1. Core philosophy

- Higher-timeframe levels (previous day high/low, range extremes, period opens) are
  **liquidity** — magnets and reversal zones where "retail ends and smart money begins" (p5).
- The tradeable event is: liquidity gets **swept**, structure **shifts** the other way, and price
  **retests** the origin of the shift (order block / imbalance / fib zone) before continuing.
  This is packaged as the "M" (buy-to-sell, bearish) and "W" (sell-to-buy, bullish) models
  (pp. 23–24), which reference every prior lesson as "pieces of confluence".
- Patience/selectivity is repeatedly stressed: "Stay out of range-bounding trading. Wait for
  clean breaks. Then move." (p7); "No setup? No trade. Simple!" (p12); "Patience makes you look
  lucky. Wait for the price to come to you. Don't chase." (p20); "Wait for retest or regret the
  fill" (p21); "You don't need more trades. You need better ones." (p18).

## 2. Concepts, as the book defines them

### 2.1 PDH/PDL — Previous Day High / Low (Lesson 1, p5)
- "PDH is the **high** of the previous day. PDL is the **low** of the previous day. Both act as
  a magnet or reversal zones." (p5)
- "Price often reacts to these areas — either rejecting them or bouncing. It also acts as a
  confluence for continuation. **Price should wick off these levels — not fully break and close
  through them.**" (p5). I.e., a wick through PDH/PDL with a close back inside is the expected
  (rejection/sweep) behavior; a full body close through is a genuine break.
- Chart detail (p5): PDH is drawn at the high of yesterday's daily candle including the wick;
  same for PDL.

### 2.2 Identifying the Range (Lesson 2, pp. 7–8)
- "Stick to the **higher timeframes — the 4HR or Daily**." (p7)
- "Find your area of resistance and your area of support — the highs and lows that matter...
  **Mark the highs and lows that need to be broken to signal direction.**" (p7)
- On $NQ "you'll usually have larger ranges because of the volatility" (p7).
- Purpose: stay out of "choppy zones where price moves without clear intent"; "Stay out of
  range-bounding trading. Wait for clean breaks." (p7)
- Worked example, Steps I–VI (pp. 7–8), a short play:
  - Step I (p7): Range high and low marked (dashed lines box the consolidation). A green "W"
    line (Weekly Open) sits mid-range. "3) Once the range breaks, this is the next low to
    target" — an external low below the range is pre-marked as the target.
  - Step II (p7): a large down candle "Broke the range" (body closes below range low);
    annotation "2) Targeting next low".
  - Step III (p8): price "Tapped the Next Low and acted as support" — the external low is hit
    and bounces.
  - Step IV (p8): "Now that it broke the range, it will target the created Order Block and
    Imbalance" — the breakdown leg left an OB (red box, upper zone just under the range high)
    and an Imbalance (blue box directly below the OB, straddling the Weekly Open).
  - Step V (p8): "Tapped the Order Block, Imbalance, and Weekly Open, as extra confluence
    **(This is where you get in Sells)**. Retested the zone to cause the External Break." The
    retrace up into OB+IMB+Weekly-Open confluence is the short entry; target line labeled
    "External Break Target" is drawn at/below the prior external low.
  - Step VI (p8): "Target Hit... These are the beautiful plays you should aim for. This is an
    **A+ setup**."
- Note the target logic: after a range break, first target = the pre-existing external low
  ("next low"); after the retest entry, target = "External Break Target" (a break *beyond* that
  external low).

### 2.3 Highs & Lows (Lesson 3, pp. 10–11)
The book does NOT define swings by N-bar pivots. It defines the *significant* high/low causally:
- Marking Highs: the High is "the **last up move that pushed us down**" (p10) — the top of the
  final rally before a decline.
- Marking Lows: the Low is "the **last down move that caused the break to the upside**" (p10),
  and equivalently "last down move that pushed us up" (p11) — the origin low of the leg that
  broke a high.
- "Both in Play" (p10): 1) a low acts as support; 2) "The low broke and market shifted bearish";
  3) "After the low broke, the new high can be used as a shift to bullish market." Speech bubble:
  the New High is marked there "because this is the **last high that caused the break down**".
- Mirror on p11: 1) high acts as resistance; 2) "The high broke and market shifted bullish";
  3) "After the high broke, the new low can be used as a shift to bearish market" — the New Low
  is "the last low that caused the break up".
- Take-away: after every structural break, you immediately re-mark the *opposing* swing (the one
  that caused the break) as the new trigger level for a shift back the other way. This recursive
  bookkeeping is the backbone of CHoCH/MSS below.
- Motto: "You won't get to the Highs without being in the Lows first" (pp. 10–11).

### 2.4 Change of Character — CHoCH (Lesson 4, p13)
- "A Change of Character is the change in price delivery that signals a **potential** shift in
  market direction. It marks the moment when the previous trend structure breaks — flipping from
  bullish to bearish or vice versa. **CHoCH is the first clue** that momentum is shifting." (p13)
- Precise trigger: "**Shift Down** is the **low that caused the new high** gets broken.
  **Shift Up** is the **high that caused the new low** gets broken." (p13)
- Chart detail (p13): in both panels the CHoCH level is broken by a single large candle whose
  *body closes through* the level (not a wick-only poke).
- "CHoCH is the signal. Action is the response." (p13)

### 2.5 Market Structure Shift — MSS (Lesson 5, p15)
- "A Market Structure Shift happens when the new low that created a new high gets broken, **the
  candle has to close above** [sic — i.e., the candle must close beyond the broken level]. It
  means that the market structure shifted **or confirms trend continuation**. It comes **after a
  CHoCH**." (p15)
- "Shift Up is when the previous **high** gets broken, continuing the uptrend. Shift Down is
  when the previous **low** gets broken, continuing the downtrend." (p15)
- Chart detail (p15): panel I labels "Previous High" then "BoS" (break of structure) where a
  candle closes above it after a "New Low" was formed; panel II mirrors with "Previous Low" /
  "BoS" after a "New High". So the book uses MSS/BoS both for reversal confirmation (after a
  CHoCH) and for trend continuation breaks.
- "Structure broke. So should your hesitation." (p15)
- Distinction as taught: CHoCH = first, tentative break of the causal swing; MSS = the
  close-confirmed break that you act on. Both are close-based per p15 wording and Add-ons #2/#3
  (p33).

### 2.6 Order Blocks — OB (Lesson 6, p17)
- "An Order Block form[s] right after a **Break in Structure**, most of the time. That is
  because strong buying or selling pressure creates Imbalances (IMB) — fast moves that push the
  price away. **The candle before that move — where orders were likely placed — becomes the
  Order Block.** It's the origin of the move, and price often **returns to it** before
  continuing in direction." (p17)
- Bearish OB chart (p17): sequence 1) Break in Structure (Shift Down) — a low is closed below;
  2) "Order Block formed" — the zone is drawn on the last *up* (blue) candle before the down
  impulse, box spanning that candle's full high-to-low range, extended right; 3) "Retested the
  Order Block" — a later wick pokes up into the zone; 4) "Continued in direction" (down). The
  origin swing is annotated "Last down move that caused the push up (Lesson 3)".
- Bullish OB chart (p17): mirror — after Shift Up, the OB is the last *down* (dark) candle
  before the up impulse; retest from above, then continuation up.
- "Find the source. Find the trade." (p17)

### 2.7 Imbalance — IMB (Lesson 7, p19)
- "Imbalance is a price area where buying and selling were not equal — caused by a strong, fast
  move. **It creates a gap between wicks** and gets revisited later as a **rejection zone or to
  fill it**." (p19) — i.e., a three-candle wick gap (what the industry calls a fair value gap):
  for a down move, the gap between candle A's low wick and candle C's high wick.
- The p19 chart shows a bearish IMB zone drawn across the fast breakdown area; "it rejected and
  continued lower, showing that the imbalance could be used as **another confluence for your
  setup**." (p19)
- "If it left fast, it'll come back slow." (p19)

### 2.8 Fibonacci Tool (Lesson 8, p21)
- "Fib levels are used to find **retest zones after shifts o[r] breaks**. They help spot where
  the price might pull back before continuing the trend." (p21)
- Anchoring: "**For Shift Up, draw fibs from low to high. For Shift Down, draw fibs from high to
  low. Candle Body to Candle Body, not wicks.**" (p21)
- Levels drawn on the tool (p21 charts): 0.00%, 50.00%, 62.00%, 70.50%, 79.00%, 100.00%.
- Named zones: "**79.00% is Premium — 70.50% is Optimal — 50.00% is Discount**" (p21).
  Percentages measure the depth of the pullback: 0% = the extreme the leg finished at, 100% = the
  leg origin. So 79% is the *deepest* acceptable pullback (closest to the origin/stop → best
  price, "Premium"), 70.5% the sweet spot, 50% the shallowest ("Discount").
- Chart detail, left panel "Retest for Upside" (p21): a CHoCH level (dashed) is broken to the
  upside by a large blue candle; the fib is drawn over that impulse leg — 100% at the leg's
  origin low (body), 0% at the leg's high (body); price pulls back into the 50% ("tapped
  discount") and rallies away. Right panel "Retest for Downside": bearish mirror; price pulls
  back up to the 70.5% ("tapped optimal") and sells off.
- "Wait for retest or regret the fill." (p21)

### 2.9 The trade models: Buy to Sell (M) and Sell to Buy (W) (Lesson 9, pp. 23–24)
These are the complete playbook, assembling all prior lessons.

**M model — bearish (p23):**
- "You wait for buyers to push price up into a retest zone to sell it off. This model forms
  **after liquidity is swept** and a **structure shift to the downside** happens. Along the way,
  an **Order Block and Imbalance usually form during the break**. You then use the other
  confirmations — structure shift, order block, imbalance, PDH/PDL — as **pieces of confluence**
  to find your best entry with minimal risk. **Risk is placed above the high that created the
  structure shift.**" (p23)
- Annotated sequence on the candle chart (p23):
  1. "Previous High" (a marked swing high).
  2. "Structure Shifted Bullish **(Liquidity Sweep)**" — a large candle takes out the Previous
     High. This upside break IS the sweep.
  3. "New Low that caused the Shift Up" — the origin low of the sweep leg is marked.
  4. "Structure Shifted Bearish" — price closes below that New Low.
  5. "New High that caused the Shift Down" — the sweep's top; annotated in red: "**Your
     Stop-Loss is close above this High**".
  6. "Tapped into Imbalance **(This is where you execute sells)**" — the IMB box (drawn over the
     breakdown leg, upper half of the leg) is retested from below; that tap is the entry.
  7. "Enjoy Profits" — brace spans the continuation down well beyond the New Low.
- Line-chart panel (p23): the path high → New High (sweep) → down through New Low → pullback
  into IMB → lower = the letter "M".

**W model — bullish (p24):** exact mirror.
- "This model forms after **sweeping liquidity** and creating a **structure shift to the
  upside**... **Risk is placed below the low that caused the structure shift.**" (p24)
- Sequence: 1) Previous Low; 2) Structure Shifted Bearish (Liquidity Sweep) below it; 3) New
  High that caused the Shift Down; 4) Structure Shifted Bullish (close above that New High);
  5) New Low that caused the Shift Up — "Your Stop-Loss is close below this Low"; 6) Tapped into
  Imbalance — "This is where you execute buys"; 7) Enjoy Profits.

### 2.10 Momentum Trades (Lesson 10, p26)
- "A Momentum move happens when price makes a strong push in one direction, **usually after
  clearing imbalance**. For a Momentum Trade to the downside, price first taps into an
  imbalance, filling the gap where orders were left behind. Then, **once the low breaks, it
  confirms continuation** to the downside. The imbalance being filled first is a strong sign
  that no orders were left unfilled — showing clean delivery and intentional movement." (p26)
- I.e., a continuation entry: IMB above gets filled → then a break of the low ⇒ go with the
  break. "Small hesitations kill big moves." (p26)

### 2.11 Opens (Lesson 11, pp. 28–29)
- "**Daily Open** is the price level where the market opened for the day. It often acts as a
  dynamic support or resistance level. **When the price is above the Daily Open, the level may
  act as support. When the price is below the Daily Open, the level may act as resistance.**"
  (p28) "Where the day begins, bias is born." (p28)
- "**Weekly, Monthly, Yearly Open** levels are key higher-timeframe reference points... like the
  Daily Open... but with **stronger impact**... **When the price is above these opens, it
  suggest[s] bullish control. When the price is below, it often reflects bearish control.** They
  act as magnets, reaction zones, and can align with major reversals or continuation plays."
  (p29) "Each open marks control." (p29)
- Note the Weekly Open ("W" green line) is used as entry confluence in the Lesson 2 worked
  example (p8, Step V).

### 2.12 Outside / Inside Bars (Lesson 12, p31)
- Defined **relative to PDH/PDL**, on "Timeframe: D, W, M" (all four chart panels, p31).
- "An **Outside Bar** is a big candle that goes above and below the high and low of the day
  before (PDH/PDL). It breaks both ends — the top and the bottom. This means the market is very
  active and **might be trying to trick people** before making a big move." (p31)
- "An **Inside Bar** is a small candle that stays inside the high and low of the day before
  (PDH/PDL). It must stay within the zone to be a valid inside bar. Sometimes, there are double
  inside daily bars. An Inside Bar indicates the **strong signal of continuation in direction**,
  up or down." (p31)
- "Silence builds strength. Breaks create direction." (p31)

## 3. Timeframe hierarchy (Mastery Add-on #1, p33)

> "**5min, 15min, and 30min are fast entry timeframes. 1HR and 4HR control intraday structure.
> Daily, Weekly, and Monthly timeframes control major bias and direction. Always align lower
> timeframe trades with higher timeframe structure.**" (p33)

Combined with the lessons:
- Daily/Weekly/Monthly: PDH/PDL (p5), period opens (pp. 28–29), inside/outside bars (p31) →
  major bias, liquidity levels, targets.
- 4H/1H: range identification (p7 explicitly says 4H or Daily), intraday structure.
- 5m/15m/30m: where the M/W entry model is executed.
The book never states one exact timeframe per model step; that is left to the trader.

## 4. Stops, targets, management

- Stop location: M model — "Risk is placed **above the high that created the structure shift**";
  "Your Stop-Loss is **close above** this High" (p23). W model — "below the low that caused the
  structure shift"; "close below this Low" (p24). No numeric buffer given.
- Stop semantics: "Always use a stop loss that **respects the candle close, not the wick**. You
  only get stopped out if the candle closes above or below your key level. **Wicks are noise.
  Closes show real break.**" (Add-on #2, p33) and "Wicks can take your stop without changing
  real direction... **Always trust the close over the wick** to avoid getting wicked out before
  hitting your target." (Add-on #3, p33). This is in tension with a resting hard stop — the book
  is teaching close-confirmed invalidation.
- Targets: never numeric. Taught targets are structural/liquidity-based: "next low to target"
  after a range break, then "External Break Target" (pp. 7–8); PDH/PDL as magnets (p5); opens as
  magnets (p29); M/W charts just show "Enjoy Profits" running well beyond the broken swing
  (pp. 23–24). No R:R ratio, partials, break-even, or trailing rules appear anywhere.
- No scaling in/out rules anywhere in the book.
- No max-hold/time-stop rules.

## 5. Risk rules and sizing (p33)

- "Risk **0.5% to 1% per trade**. Small risk keeps you alive during drawdowns. Protect your
  capital first — profits come second." (Add-on #4)
- "If you hit a **losing streak, stop trading**... Step away, clear your mind, and review."
  (Add-on #5)
- Context is prop-firm futures trading: "$50,000 Prop Firm account is much better than $100,000
  or $150,000 accounts" (p30); "Most Prop Firms let you copy trade up to 5 accounts at once"
  (p14); author bio touts "$850,000+ secured in Prop Firm funding" (p35). No contract-count
  guidance.

## 6. Session / time-of-day / news

- **No explicit session windows are taught.** Circumstantial: the p32 phone mock shows 9:30
  (NYSE open) with the message "Use alerts, not emotions"; the instrument focus is $NQ (pp. 7,
  33).
- News rule: "**Wait for news to release before taking trades.** News events can cause fake
  breakouts and fast spikes. Let the move happen — then trade the real direction after."
  (Add-on #8, p33)
- Alerts: "Set alerts at key levels. Alerts keep you patient and protect you from overtrading.
  Let price come to you." (Add-on #6, p33)

## 7. Cross-market confirmation (Add-on #7, p33)

> "$NQ moves with $ES, $QQQ, and $SPY. Watching these tickers gives confirmation. **If they move
> together, your setup is stronger. If they diverge, be cautious and stay patient.**" (p33)

## 8. Things the book emphasizes as critical / common mistakes

- Trust candle **closes**, not wicks, for every break decision (p33 #2, #3; p15 "the candle has
  to close"; p5 wick-off vs close-through distinction).
- Don't trade inside the range; wait for the clean break and the retest (pp. 7, 20, 21).
- Don't chase; let price come to your level via alerts (pp. 20, 32, 33 #6).
- No setup = no trade (p12); quality over quantity (p18).
- Most traders "don't even mark PDH/PDL and miss the cleanest liquidity grabs" (p25).
- Wait out news (p33 #8). Keep the chart clean — no indicator clutter (p33 #9). Don't revenge
  trade (p33 #10).

## 9. What the book does NOT specify (relevant for mechanization)

- No numeric swing/pivot definition (all swings are causal: "the low that caused the new high").
- No exact timeframe assignment for the M/W model steps (only the p33 tiering).
- No numeric take-profit, R:R, partials, break-even, trailing, or time-stop rules.
- No session window.
- No explicit rule for when a marked level/fib expires other than it being broken by a close.
- No definition of how many confluences make a setup "A+" — entry quality is discretionary
  ("pieces of confluence to find your best entry", p23).
